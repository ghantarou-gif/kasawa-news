Workers from countries around the world, including France, Poland, the UK, the US, Japan, Brazil and South Africa, are staging a global strike in their Amazon workplaces demanding better wages and labour rights.

For the seventh consecutive Black Friday, Amazon warehouses, delivery stations and offices are set to run at a slow rate as 50 organisations — trade unions, environmental movements and tax justice campaigners — launch Make Amazon Pay strikes.

Strikes are staged from Black Friday to Cyber Monday in some countries, the busiest shopping period of the year for the retail giant.

In Germany, United Services Union (ver.di) called for strikes in 11 Amazon workplaces on Friday, while unions have managed to rally in at least 23 locations across India.

"Despite massive intimidation, Amazon employees are striking for recognition of the collective agreements for the retail sector and a collective agreement on good and healthy work," said the United Services Trade Union, reported by Sky News .
