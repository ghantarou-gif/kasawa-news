Pope Leo XIV has become the first pope to vote in a U.S. general election.

The Vatican confirmed Friday that pontiff had submitted a mail-in ballot in Chicago ahead of the Nov. 3 midterm elections, citing his long-standing belief in civic participation.

Leo, formerly Robert Prevost, "has exercised his right to vote as a United States citizen" and has done so "whenever he was eligible to do so," the Holy See press office said.

"The Holy Father expresses his belief in the right and the duty of citizens to participate in democratic processes," it added.
