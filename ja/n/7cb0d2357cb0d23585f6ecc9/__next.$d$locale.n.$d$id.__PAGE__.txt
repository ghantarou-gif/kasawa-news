What are the next steps after Israel election ends in deadlock? Israel's consequential election has ended in a deadlock, with neither side able to claim a majority, What happens now?

Israeli left-wing activists call for a government with Arab parties outside Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot's residence in Herzliya, Israel, Thursday.

TEL AVIV, Israel — Israel's election has produced a political deadlock, leaving neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu nor his main challenger, Gadi Eisenkot, with a clear path to forming a government.

The final results announced Friday give opposition parties more seats than Netanyahu's right-wing bloc. But Eisenkot, a center-right former military chief of staff, cannot reach a governing majority without the support of predominantly Arab parties — an option Eisenkot's allies repeatedly ruled out during the campaign.

Netanyahu's Likud party received the most votes — winning 27 seats — but his bloc lost the majority it had before the election. The coalition of right-wing, far-right and ultra-Orthodox parties that supported his government won a combined 53 seats.
