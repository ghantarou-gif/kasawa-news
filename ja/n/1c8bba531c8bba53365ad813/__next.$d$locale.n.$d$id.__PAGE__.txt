Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva defeated his right-wing rival Flávio Bolsonaro in a bitterly fought campaign by 4.4m votes.

Bolsonaro has told his supporters in Brasilia that he would seek the annulment of the election. But this has not stopped Lula and his supporters from celebrating what they describe as a "historic victory".

It was a dramatic comeback after the former metalworker, who has already served three terms as president, lost the first round of voting.

Many had not thought it possible for him to turn the result around in just three weeks. But Lula's campaign sprang back, driven by street demonstrations and a massive social media push.

His election win has bucked a wider trend in Latin America, where voters have punished incumbents and turned to the right. Across the region, large countries such as Argentina, Chile and Colombia all now have conservative leaders at their helm.
