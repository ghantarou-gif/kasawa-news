5:T4ad,A new deal between the UK and the EU on food, plants and animals will "make a real positive difference for consumers, businesses and farmers" in Northern Ireland Stormont's agriculture minister has said.

The agreement was confirmed on Friday afternoon when Prime Minister Andy Burnham met European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in Brussels.

Since Brexit, Northern Ireland has continued to follow EU rules for agri-food products while the rest of the UK has not.

The new deal will see the whole UK realigning with EU rules on those products, meaning goods being sent from GB to NI will no longer need to be checked at ports.

It will also mean a reduction but not elimination of the paperwork required to move agri-food goods from Great Britain to Northern Ireland.

The intention is to implement the deal from March 2028, though that could be dependent on the UK agreeing to reduce tuition fees for EU students.

The minister Andrew Muir, said: "Whilst this will not fix everything, it's a massive step in the right direction.
