US school bus drivers decline amid low pay and poor work conditions

4 日前

The number of school bus drivers in the US has dropped 5% over the last year as drivers continue to experience low pay, poor working conditions and school budget shortfalls, according to a new report.

The total number of school bus drivers is down 15%, or nearly 34,000 drivers, compared with 2019 levels, according to an new analysis from the Economic Policy Institute.

The 5% decrease from 2025 to 2026 represents a loss of nearly 10,200 drivers and was the largest single-year decline since 2022 for a workforce that transports an estimated 26 million children daily, the largest mass transit program in the US.

Dawn Freedenburg, who has been a school bus driver for 16 years in Chicago, Illinois, said the long process to be trained and licensed to be a school bus driver can deter new drivers. New drivers must get the proper commercial drivers license (CDL) and undergo extensive training and background checks to do the job, a process which requires weeks of unpaid work.

“It isn’t easy for most people to put that much time into a new job without being paid, especially in this economy,” said Freedenburg.

And once they get the job, wages typically range from $19 to $27 an hour, but most drivers don’t average 40 hours per week during the school year and receive no pay during school breaks. Few contractors or school districts provide additional benefits such as health insurance, she added.

“My current district guarantees five hours per day for 174 school days a year, but our work can fall anywhere between 5.45am and 5pm depending on your route assignments,” she added. “There is the possibility of extra work like field trips sometimes, but there are typically more drivers wanting them then trips available.”

The decline and ongoing shortages of school bus drivers have often forced school districts to cut and consolidate bus routes. Eighty-percent of school districts in the US have been affected by bus driver shortages, with 73% experiencing shortages from budget shortfalls, according to a 2025 report from the Associated Press-NORC Center for Public Affairs Research .

Higher diesel costs amid the war with Iran and Russia’s war on Ukraine have worsened budget issues, costing school districts an estimated $1.1bn more from November 2025 to October 2026 compared to the previous year.

Wages for school bus drivers also dropped in September 2026 by 0.7% from September 2025 after accounting for inflation, while pay for other bus drivers has remained steady. Median hourly pay for school bus drivers was $22.49 in 2026, compared to a median hourly pay of $23.89 for all bus drivers. School bus drivers comprise 39.4% of all bus drivers.

Drivers also say that student behavior has also worsened since the Covid-19 pandemic, and school bus drivers are often caught in between the administration and parents, making it difficult to address behavioral issues.

Peter Sandler began working as a school bus driver in 2020 in Connecticut after retiring in 2016, noting that many bus drivers are retirees, which contributes to high turnover rates of school bus drivers.

As a result of bus driver shortages, his former contractor consolidated bus routes and increased the number of children per bus.

“That made buses filled to capacity which makes the driver’s life a nightmare,” Sandler said. “Every parent should be forced to see how their children act when they ride the school bus.”

Sandler also noted that with only 5.5 hours of paid work a day and little work when school is out of session, it is economically unfeasible to make ends meet as a school bus driver.

“If you add in summer work and taking charter work, its difficult to get past $40,000 annually,” he added. “Obviously, that’s not a lot of money, especially in a region where a one bedroom apartment would set you back $1,600 to $1,700 a month in the cheapest communities, and over $2,000 a month in better areas.”

Antonia Namnath, a school bus driver in the Beaverton school district in Oregon explained the school bus driver shortage isn’t an issue in her school district because the drivers are paid good wages, with starting wages ranging from $26 to $33 an hour, with annual increases tied to a union contract with the Oregon School Employees Association (OSEA Chapter 48).

“We get a good wage, incredible benefits, health benefits, vision, dental, everything,” she noted.

“The drivers have to be greatly compensated for the responsibility they’re taking on, which is to transport children safely from point A to point B, and manage the students in a safe way.”

Melissa Fay France, a school bus driver outside of Kalamazoo, Michigan, said the lack of medical insurance and low pay often make leaving the profession an attractive option for drivers.

With the “responsibility there is being a driver and maintaining your CDL, those wages need to match that,” France said. “I am a great driver, both on the road and for the kids. I know my worth and the schools aren’t living up to their end.”
