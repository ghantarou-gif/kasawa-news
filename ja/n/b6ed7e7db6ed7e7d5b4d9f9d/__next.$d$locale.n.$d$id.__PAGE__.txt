5:T486,Bosnia and Herzegovina went to the polls on October 4 with new technology that was meant to end years of vote-rigging.

Chief among the abuses was the casting of ballots in the name of deceased voters who had lingered for years on outdated voter rolls.

At just over 40%, turnout was the lowest since the Bosnian War ended in 1995, in part because the old tricks no longer worked.

In earlier elections, allegations kept surfacing that votes were being cast in the name of people who had died or long since moved abroad. Others said voters cast their ballot multiple times or that blank ballots were added after polls closed to make the turnout look higher.

The voter registry, packed with names of people who had passed away or left the country, was the perfect tool for all of this.

This time, however, every polling station used biometric fingerprint checks.

This represented a clear step forward in making voter identification far harder to manipulate.
