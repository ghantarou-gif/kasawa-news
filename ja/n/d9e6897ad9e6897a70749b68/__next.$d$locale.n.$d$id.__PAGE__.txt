This week, the Cheesecake Factory restaurant chain unexpectedly found itself in the political discourse.

During a campaign event in Ohio, former President Obama criticized President Trump's affection for gold decor, saying, "He's got the White House looking like Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory."

Trump's Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent retaliated by having a French Dip Cheeseburger for lunch at a Cheesecake Factory near the White House, telling reporters, "I had a great meal, always have a great meal. I would tell the Martha's Vineyard set to give it a try."

But this latest partisan debate made me wonder. Why spend $600 million or more to build a new White House ballroom to host state dinners? Why not just host those esteemed events instead at the Cheesecake Factory?

Come to a state dinner, I've been told, and you kind of have to gulp down whatever yellow squash velouté or fricassée of fava beans a server slides under your chin.

But isn't America all about freedom, choice and options galore?
