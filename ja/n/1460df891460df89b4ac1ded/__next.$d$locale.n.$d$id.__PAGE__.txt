There are three forms of plague, all caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis.

The three forms of plague are bubonic, septicemic and pneumonic.

Bubonic plague is the most common form; it does not spread from person to person. If untreated, the bacteria can move through the body and bloodstream, causing septicemic plague, which causes internal bleeding and organ failure.

If the bacteria reach the lungs, they cause pneumonic plague. Pneumonic plague is highly contagious because it can pass from person to person through the air, for example, via coughing.

If untreated, bubonic plague kills between 30-60% of patients. The septicemic and pneumonic forms are always fatal without treatment.
