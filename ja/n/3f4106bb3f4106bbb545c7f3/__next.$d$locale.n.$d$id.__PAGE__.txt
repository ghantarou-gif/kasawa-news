1b:Td1e,Donald Trump has been granted a temporary delay before he must give a witness statement in his $10bn defamation lawsuit against the BBC.

In late October, Enjoliqué Lett, a federal magistrate judge, gave Trump a firm 11 December deadline to sit for a deposition by lawyers representing the BBC in his lawsuit against the British broadcaster. If he failed to do so by that date, he could be subject to penalties up to dismissal of the case, the judge warned.

But on Tuesday, the president got something of a reprieve when Jeffrey Kuntz, a federal district court judge, approved a joint motion by the parties and extended the deadline for Trump’s deposition until 22 January. The judge also pushed back the deadline for discovery in the case from 18 December to 29 January.

Trump had originally agreed to sit for a deposition on 16 October, but his team backed out, citing “matters of national importance”. That led the BBC to file a motion to compel Trump to sit for a deposition in a timely fashion.

According to the joint motion filed on Monday, the BBC has agreed to the Trump team’s proposal for the president to sit for a deposition between 11 January and 22 January after his representatives said he was unavailable to do so this year “due to his schedule and the nature of his profession”. As that timeline nears, the parties will meet to select a date and identify a secure location for the deposition.

“It is Trump’s case, so he should expect to be deposed,” Kuntz wrote in his order. “It appears he acknowledges that reality.”

The judge ordered the parties to schedule Trump’s deposition by Friday at noon. Kuntz scheduled a trial date for the case for 3 May 2027 in Fort Lauderdale, Florida.

So far, Trump’s lawyers have deposed seven defendant witnesses. As part of the agreement between Trump and the BBC, the president’s lawyers will not depose Samir Shah, the BBC chair, or a former BBC director-general – nor any current or former BBC employee – until the president sits for questioning.

While Trump’s seven-hour-long deposition will be videotaped, it will only be available to attorneys for the parties.

In addition to Trump, the BBC might also be able to depose his daughter, Ivanka Trump, his son, Donald Trump Jr, and his son-in-law Jared Kushner. “Defendant is currently conferring with President Trump and these non-parties regarding the subpoenas directed to them, given that these non-parties may be objecting to, or moving to quash, those subpoenas,” the parties wrote in their joint motion.

Last week, Kuntz, a Trump appointee, refused the BBC’s request to dismiss the case , though he threw out the president’s contention that the broadcaster violated Florida’s Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act.

The lawsuit stems from a Panorama documentary aired by the BBC that spliced together two comments made nearly an hour apart by Trump during a rally on 6 January 2021, before the US Capitol riot. The BBC has acknowledged that this specific edit was misleading, but Trump has gone on to claim, falsely, that the BBC did not just edit his speech but used AI to insert words into his speech.
