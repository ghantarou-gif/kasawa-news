Hungarian authorities said they seized more than €17.5 million in cash buried in forests and hidden in other places across the country this week.

On 12 and 13 November, the National Asset Recovery and Protection Office (NVVH) carried out coordinated searches at multiple locations across Hungary, including in Budapest and the counties of Pest, Békés and Nógrád, as part of a criminal investigation involving Miklós Seszták, a former minister of national development.

During the searches, investigators uncovered large amounts of cash hidden in a variety of spots: buried in forests, concealed in agricultural buildings and warehouses, stashed inside residential properties and stored in a high-value safe in a remote location.

Over the course of the two-day operation, investigators recovered a total of 5,071,632,000 forints, €2,658,350 and $1,257,100. On Friday, NVVH staff paid the cash into the Hungarian State Treasury.
