Estonia formally approved the closure of all road crossings on its border with Russia from 1 December, in response to a sabotage attack on a defence company that Tallinn attributed to Russian military intelligence.

All five checkpoints — Narva-1, Narva-2, Koidula, Luhamaa and Saatse — will be closed. The government said it may review the decision in six months. Rail services will not be affected.

The government described the move as "a response to Russia's ongoing hostile actions against Estonia and other European countries." Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Estonia would "not allow Russia to use our openness against us."

The closure follows an attempted arson at the Threod Systems defence facility in Viimsi, near Tallinn, on the night of 14 November. Production and products were undamaged.

Authorities detained eight people from Latvia in connection with the incident.
