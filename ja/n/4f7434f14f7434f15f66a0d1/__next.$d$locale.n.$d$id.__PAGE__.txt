The climate emergency is an immediate crisis for refugees, with three in four – nearly 90 million people – living in countries facing high to severe exposure to hazards such as drought, floods and extreme heat.

Vulnerable as they are, climate financing is failing to reach many communities at risk, the UN refugee agency, UNHCR , warned in a report published on Friday ahead of the COP31 climate change conference opening next week in Antalya, Türkiye.

“Refugees face a double injustice. Forced to flee conflicts they did not cause, they are now on the frontlines of a climate crisis they have contributed least to,” said Antón Leis García, Assistant High Commissioner for Operations.

“For millions, climate change is not a distant threat. It is destroying shelters, undermining livelihoods and putting their hopes of rebuilding their lives at risk.”

The report – No Escape III – finds that the fragile and conflict-affected countries that host large refugee populations receive only around 10 per cent of global funding to address climate change.
