Cybersecurity researchers say they detected hackers probing internet-connected refrigeration systems across Europe in the days before a wave of cyberattacks and incidents disrupted supermarkets and blood banks, with evidence suggesting it may have been part of a coordinated operation.

Refrigeration systems in supermarkets across nine European countries as well as several blood banks in France were attacked on 2 and 3 November.

Hackers exploited weak cybersecurity and vulnerabilities in manufacturer Danfoss’s AK-SM refrigeration system manager to put fridges and freezers on defrost settings, in some cases temperatures were set as high 50 degrees Celsius.

The incidents forced many grocery stores to throw away spoiled produce from their fridges and freezers and have become a very visible example of how easily cyberattacks can disrupt everyday life by targeting infrastructure systems that are often taken for granted.
