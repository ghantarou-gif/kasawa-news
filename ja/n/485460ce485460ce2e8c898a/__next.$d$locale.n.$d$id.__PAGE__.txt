5:T544,After an eight-year journey travelling almost six billion miles (10bn km), a spacecraft called BepiColombo has arrived at its final destination and is now in orbit around Mercury.

It‘s operating in an extremely hostile environment: Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun and temperatures can reach a fierce 430C and then plunge to -180C, and it is constantly blasted with intense solar radiation.

Until now, the planet has been little explored but the hope is this mission will solve some of Mercury's major mysteries.

These include uncovering why there is ice in its craters despite its proximity to the Sun, and understanding the mysterious hollows that are dotted all over its surface.

"I think it's a little bit of an under-loved planet in the inner Solar System, but I think that's one of the reasons why it is so compelling," said Prof Emma Bunce, from Leicester University, who is a principal investigator on the mission.

"There are lots of reasons, scientifically, why we really want to understand Mercury, how it formed, how it evolved, what it looks like today, but also how it interacts with the Sun and the extreme space weather that it experiences."
