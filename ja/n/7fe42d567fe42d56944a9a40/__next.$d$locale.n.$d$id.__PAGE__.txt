2b:T18df,Relatives of gravely ill detainees at two Florida immigration jails say authorities are about to deport their loved ones to unknown countries in Africa despite worsening medical conditions.

Flights are scheduled as early as Wednesday, according to the families and the immigration advocacy group the Workers Circle, which has been monitoring conditions and speaking with detainees inside two Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) facilities in south Florida.

The group said multiple sick detainees with varying conditions had been targeted for imminent deportation, and learned about the operation only in the last few days.

“I received a call from several detainees letting me know that men were being pulled in to interviews with deportation agents, where they were being informed that because of their medical conditions, Mexico would not be accepting their entry, and they will be sent to Africa ,” Arianne Betancourt, the group’s immigrant rights organizer, said at a Tuesday-night press conference.

“We know that once they are deported and sent to these third countries, they are not receiving medical care in detention. They have difficulties getting medicine.”

A Department of Homeland Security (DHS) spokesperson, in a statement to the Guardian late on Wednesday, said it was “a false story designed to smear the brave men and women of ICE as they are facing a more than 1,600% increase in assaults against them”.

“No sick detainees are being deported. All detainees are medically cleared before travel and leave with any necessary medications.”

The affected detainees are at the Krome North processing center in Miami, which was found to be experiencing severe overcrowding by a government inspector’s report last month, and the Broward transitional center in Pompano Beach. Both facilities are privately run on behalf of ICE.

Anisley Cortés, the wife of Noslén Sendra, a 41-year-old Cuban national, said her husband had been incarcerated since last November, first at the now closed “ Alligator Alcatraz ” immigration jail, and at Krome since April. She said her husband has uncontrolled diabetes and acute pancreatitis that resulted in a weeks-long hospital stay earlier this year.

“He lost over 50 pounds. They wouldn’t give him his medication, and they would not give him enough food. It was a nightmare of a time for all of us,” she said. “He is very sick and needs the right healthcare.

“Cuba will not accept him as a deportee, and we now have proof that Mexico will not accept him as a deportee because he is so sick. And so they told him that because he could not be accepted at either of those two countries, ‘Oh well, we’re going to have to send you to Africa.’

“They told him that Mexico would not accept him because he has too many medications that he requires, and so they said, ‘Well, we have to deport you anyway.’ We’re left with the uncertainty of not knowing how he is, or if we’ll see him again.”

Shalyn Fluharty, an immigration rights attorney, said information from the detainees indicated deportation flights were imminent.

“We understand that just this week, within a couple of days, likely Thursday and Friday, there will be two flights going to Africa, in particular to the Central African Republic, and we also believe Burundi ,” she said.

Fluharty said previously deported immigrants were shackled at their hands, ankles and waists during lengthy flights, and some who complained were placed in full body wraps with no ability to move.

“This is what we anticipate will be happening this week for these individuals, based upon what we’ve seen in the past,” she said.

“Travel can last sometimes up to 24 hours. What if you’re somebody who has a heart condition? Who has a high risk of stroke? Who is experiencing diabetes and requires regular access to food or insulin? These conditions take your already vulnerable medical state and make them, unfortunately, often life-threatening.”

The DHS statement said Sendra was “a criminal illegal alien from Cuba convicted of fraud, larceny, and property crimes” and “has been consistently cleared and discharged from the hospital following medical evaluation”.

He was ordered removed from the US in September 2015, it said.

“Third country removals are an essential public safety tool that remove the most dangerous criminal illegal aliens from our streets,” the spokesperson said.

“Illegal aliens being sent to a third country receive notice and assurances prior to their removal, and the Department of State is given diplomatic assurances from destination countries that the illegal aliens being removed there will be safe.”

Betancourt said families risked retaliation for their relatives by coming forward. “This is not a single one-off story,” she said.

“The inspector general’s report confirmed that there is a lack of medical care inside of Krome detention facility. Families have been demanding oversight, and every day they are experiencing real fear to speak up and advocate for their loved ones.”

During two unannounced visits to Krome in May, government inspectors found multiple violations of noncompliance with detention standards, including severe overcrowding. Holding 1,102 detainees in a facility with a maximum capacity of 882, the report said, “jeopardizes the safe and orderly operations within the facility”.

Other listed violations included an inadequate number of operational toilets, sinks and showers, and previous complaints included female detainees made to use toilets in full view of men, and arriving detainees held for more than 24 hours in a bus in the parking lot because of a lack of space.

Advocates say Donald Trump ’s sweeping second-term immigration crackdown has caused overcrowding in detention centers nationwide that the DHS has attempted to alleviate by ramping up deportation flights to so-called third countries.

The controversial practice, approved by the US supreme court last month, has seen hundreds of detainees deported to at least a dozen African countries where they have no ties.
