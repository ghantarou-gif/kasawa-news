5:T4b0,A bird named Phil has astonished conservationists by completing a 6,800-mile (10,900km) round trip from England's east coast to Siberia and back.

Bar-tailed godwits are champion fliers, migrating between Britain and the Arctic, but the precise routes used by individual birds is largely unknown.

Phil was fitted with a tiny solar-powered tracker that recorded his movements throughout the journey.

The data showed he travelled from The Wash , external to the Wadden Sea, along the coasts of the Netherlands, Germany and Denmark, before continuing to Siberia.

On his return, he crossed the Arctic Ocean to northern Norway, stopping on three islands before heading back through the Wadden Sea to The Wash.

Scientists say Phil's extraordinary travels highlight the importance of protecting the world's wetlands, which are havens for millions of migratory birds each year.

Dr Nigel Clark, of the conservation charity, The Wash Wader Research Group, said The Wash is the UK's single most important coastal wetland for migratory waders, with spectacular numbers seen in autumn and winter.
