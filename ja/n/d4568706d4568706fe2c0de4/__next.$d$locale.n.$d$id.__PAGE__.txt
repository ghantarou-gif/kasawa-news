5:T4f2,Doctors involved in a 24-hour strike over pay have warned more health staff will leave Northern Ireland for higher wages and better conditions elsewhere.

Seamus Crumley is among medics who joined a picket line in Belfast after their union, the British Medical Association (BMA), rejected a pay rise offer of 3.5%.

He moved to Northern Ireland from his native Scotland last year but told BBC News NI that despite securing a more senior role he "took a pay cut to come here".

Health Minister Robbie Butler said he would try to do "everything in my power" to implement this year's pay award - but said he was unable to do so at present in the absence of a Stormont budget .

Butler added that the recommended 3.5% rise was higher than that for other healthcare professionals working in Northern Ireland's health system.

But the BMA argues doctors' salaries fell significantly in real terms during years of below-inflation pay rises so it is seeking "pay restoration" dating back to 2008.

It has blamed current pay levels in the sector for the problems the health service is having in recruiting and retaining staff.
