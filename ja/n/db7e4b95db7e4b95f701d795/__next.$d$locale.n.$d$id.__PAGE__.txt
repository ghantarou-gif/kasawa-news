5:T4f3,As part of its drive to "streamline and simplify" EU laws, the European Commission has proposed sweeping changes for agriculture and food safety legislation. These include a move to ease rules controlling the use of pesticides and herbicides , which help fight pests and weeds.

Under current EU rules, which date back to 2011, primary pesticide components, or active substances, are only granted approval for limited periods of no more than 15 years.

The European Commission's proposed revamp of the system would make indefinite greenlighting — including of the controversial glyphosate , which has been linked to cancer and kills almost all plant life — the default. Safety reviews would only be done in exceptional cases, and regulatory authorities, not the industry, would bear responsibility.

The commission has said its goal is to cut costs, simplify work for authorities and help farmers stay competitive. Scientists and doctors, however, warn that concerns over human health and environmental impact from these toxic substances are being brushed aside.

Why does the EU want to deregulate pesticides?
