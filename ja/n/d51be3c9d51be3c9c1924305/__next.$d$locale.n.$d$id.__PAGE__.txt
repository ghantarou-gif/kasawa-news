28:T17dc,Millions of people are planning to fly in the US over the Thanksgiving holiday, just two weeks after the US Federal Aviation Administration’s 13 November ground stop halted all flights nationwide for the first time since 9/11 (aside from a brief halt in 2023).

In the chaos that ensued, some travelers embarked on overnight drives to catch cruise ships and bought new cars to make business meetings. Many had to simply cancel their plans and incur the costs that came with it. More than 18,000 flights were cancelled at a potential cost to travelers of $1.9bn in lost time and related expenses, Anderson Economic Group estimates .

The government-run system that keeps US air travel safe is so sprawling, chronically outdated and poorly managed that more flight delays and safety incidents are likely, a prescient September report from the Government Accountability Office warned. Long delays have also become much more common because airports are often at capacity and airlines are operating without any reserve staff or planes.

“Air travel in general has gotten much slower and less reliable” than it was in the 1970s, 1980s or 1990s, said Paul Hudson, the president of FlyersRights , a non-profit that advocates for passengers.

“This is the new reality,” Hudson said. “I don’t expect it is going to change in any short-term way.”

US law only requires airlines to refund passengers the full cost of flights that don’t take off or are significantly delayed. Travelers often spend hours haggling with travel insurers, credit card lenders, cruise lines, hotels, rental cars and Airbnb owners over refunds they may never get.

Experts say not all hope is lost. Some preparation and insight into how compensation works can make cancellations or delays less of a headache.

If a flight is cancelled or delayed for three hours or more, US law requires an airline to reimburse you for the flight, unless you take a voucher or accept a new reservation. But it’s hard for travelers to get compensation for other expenses like an Airbnb or rental car when a cancellation or delay isn’t an airline’s fault, as in the case of the recent ground stop.

Because the FAA ordered the stop, said Teresa Murray, consumer watchdog director at the US Public Interest Research Group, a national public advocate that publishes a guide for flyers “there really aren’t a lot of avenues to get reimbursed for other kinds of expenses.”

The Department of Transportation has a guide to how different airlines handle hotel and food vouchers during delays . PIRG notes that airlines will sometimes pay for meals or hotels, even if the issue is not their fault, if you ask.

“The most important thing is to document what happens when it happens,” said Ben Farrow, a partner with Anderson, Williams and Farrow, part of the LegalShield network that provides affordable legal advice. If you are using an airline app and get a cancellation notice, screenshot it. It’s also a good idea to keep track of flight delays and the receipts for meals bought during delays.

“If I were stuck with a pile of receipts, I’d check my credit card first,” said Christopher Elliott, whose Elliot Report advises consumers. “A lot of premium cards cover meals and hotels after a delay of six or 12 hours, and most people have no idea they have that coverage.”

But some will not cover other costs related to Friday’s incident. While Chase and Capital One cover general “equipment failure” delays related to air travel, American Express does not, consumer credit card guide JoinKudos found.

Hotel chains like Marriott don’t have to waive any fees, even if the cancellation is completely out of the customer’s control, Elliot said. But a polite call could get some fees waived, especially if you are a valued customer. While you’re at it, he added “ask the airline for a goodwill voucher. It doesn’t owe you one, but sometimes it’ll give you one just to make you go away.”

“You need to have a Plan B, particularly if your travel is time sensitive,” said Hudson.

He suggests booking a backup refundable same-day flight on a second airline to your destination, and cancelling it if your first flight is leaving on time. You should also think about alternative ground transportation, he said. And in some cases, consider having the meeting or family reunion on Zoom instead. “There are always alternatives,” he said.

And getting to a destination early makes delays and cancellations less disruptive. For Thanksgiving , Hudson said, he booked tickets to arrive four days early.

FlyersRights has a free hotline for stranded passengers to call for advice.

Some consumer advocates recommend frequent travelers buy annual travel insurance, rather than getting it on a trip-by-trip basis from airlines or hotels or third party sites.

But some unfortunate travelers found that not all travel insurance is created equal after the FAA stop. Some policies with “ cancel for any reason ” coverage paid expenses such as hotel stays, while others did not.

When there are delays and cancellations, dealing with a third party often complicates things, consumer experts say.

“If there’s an issue with needing to get rebooked, needing to get a refund, needing to change your flight, you’ve got a layer between you” and the company, said PIRG’s Murray. “You don’t want to be dealing with a middle man.”

Keep the scout motto “be prepared” in mind, says Murray. “Think about packing your personal item with things that you might need if you’re stuck,” she said, including granola bars or a piece of fruit and a few days of any daily medication you take.

If you can, fly in the morning, when there’s less time for delays to pile up, PIRG recommends . Avoid checking baggage, and if you do, put a tracker on it.

“Don’t travel the day of your event,” said Farrow. “Have a backup plan. And have cash in your pocket,” he said.
