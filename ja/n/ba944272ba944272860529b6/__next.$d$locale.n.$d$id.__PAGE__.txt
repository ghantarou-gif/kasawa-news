Ghostwriter Sally McGraw was hoping to land a job developing a book proposal for a prospective client. But after submitting a writing sample, she got an unexpected response: an email telling her that multiple artificial intelligence detection tools had flagged her work as machine-generated.

"I was floored because I had not used AI to generate the text," McGraw said. "I'd written it all myself."

Having spent more than 20 years as a professional nonfiction writer, McGraw said she was angry that her work had been dismissed as AI-written.

"You know where you see a cartoon character going red from their chin all the way up to the crown of their head? I felt my ears starting to burn," she said. "I thought, what is going on?"

McGraw said she assured the client she had written the proposal herself, but decided not to press the issue because she considered the job "minor."

Sally McGraw, a ghostwriter, said she's changed her writing style after a prospective client suggested her work was AI-generated.

In an email to NPR, the client confirmed that she had screened McGraw's work for AI. But she said the results were not the determining factor in choosing to hire a different writer, instead citing experience and fit.
