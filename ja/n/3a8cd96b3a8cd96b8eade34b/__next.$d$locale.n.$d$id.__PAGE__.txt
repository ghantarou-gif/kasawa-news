1e:T137a,The federal judge Loren L AliKhan on Wednesday granted a preliminary injunction blocking the Brendan Carr-led Federal Communications Commission (FCC) from taking any action curbing ABC’s ability to renew its broadcast licenses, after the network sued the agency in August over an unusual demand to apply early to renew the eight local licenses for stations it owns.

The FCC claimed that the early renewal process was necessary to procure documents related to an ongoing Diversity, Equity and Inclusion (DEI) investigation, but the network countered that it was being punished for editorial coverage that was deemed critical of the Trump administration . The FCC’s order came after Donald Trump and the first lady, Melania Trump, criticized ABC for a comment made by the late night host Jimmy Kimmel.

At a hearing early last month , AliKhan seemed skeptical of the FCC’s reasoning for the early renewal order.

In her ruling, the judge said that ABC is “likely to succeed on their first amendment claim” of retaliation.

In addition to granting ABC’s request for a preliminary injunction, the judge denied the FCC’s request to dismiss the case. But she also denied ABC’s request to prevent the FCC from taking “any actions, formal or informal, to coerce or threaten plaintiffs with sanctions in an effort to alter their exercise of editorial discretion”.

In her ruling, the judge acknowledged the seriousness of the possible penalties facing ABC – and the chilling effect produced by an early license renewal order on a news organization’s first amendment rights. “Given the extreme, indeed existential, nature of the possible penalty Plaintiffs face – loss of their broadcast licenses – the court concludes that early license renewal proceedings create a risk sufficient to chill a broadcaster of ordinary firmness,” she wrote. “Any broadcaster of ordinary firmness – wealthy or not – would not hesitate to think twice if faced with such an existential threat to its business.”

The judge highlighted Trump’s long-standing animus against ABC as a clear factor in the FCC’s actions. “Starting with expressions of hostility, President Trump has set his sights on Plaintiff ABC for several years now,” she wrote, adding that the chairperson, Brendan Carr, “has taken President Trump’s rhetoric as his marching orders”.

As for the timing of the early renewal order request, coming soon after Trump’s criticism of Kimmel, the judge wrote: “Against the backdrop of President Trump and Chairman Carr’s call-and-response, it is no coincidence that President Trump criticized Mr. Kimmel’s skit on social media and the very next day Defendants initiated the early license renewal proceedings.”

The judge also noted the odd timing of the early renewal order, coming more than a year after the FCC began investigating ABC. And she acknowledged as credible the network’s concern that the agency’s license review process could run indefinitely.

“The existence of other mechanisms to solicit public input, coupled with the untenable timeline, casts serious doubt on the argument that Defendants are using the early license renewal proceedings to enhance public participation,” she wrote. “All told, the ‘significant mismatch’ between Defendants’ decision to order the proceedings when they did and their stated rationale for doing so presents an air of pretext the court will not ignore.”

In a statement, an ABC News spokesperson said: “Today’s decision is an important victory for the First Amendment. We thank the court for its careful consideration.”

Anna M Gomez, the lone FCC commissioner appointed by a Democratic president, also celebrated the judge’s ruling. “This decision shows that this FCC is a paper tiger whose threats crumble when challenged by those willing to defend their rights,” she said in a statement. “For nearly two years, the agency has weaponized its licensing authority to retaliate against broadcasters for exercising their right to free expression, and now a federal court has stepped in to put a check on that conduct. That brings badly needed accountability to a Commission and an administration that have politicized the machinery of government in a sustained campaign of censorship and control. Every broadcaster facing this pressure should take note and choose courage over capitulation because, as I’ve said from the beginning, the First Amendment is on their side.”

“While we appreciate the district court denying in part Disney’s request for preliminary injunctive relief, the portion of the decision granting Disney’s motion is wrong on the facts and wrong on the law – as controlling Supreme Court precedent makes clear,” a FCC spokesperson said in a statement. “The FCC looks forward to prevailing in full on this matter.
