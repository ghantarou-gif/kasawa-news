As the Coalition stares down One Nation in NSW, a new threat is rising: independents

22 時間前

The New South Wales Coalition is facing a renewed threat to its heartland seats on Sydney’s north shore.

This time, the threat is from independent candidates – and they’re backed by the same community activists who put a swathe of teal independents into federal parliament.

The challenge, which has so far remained under the radar, comes as the NSW Coalition tries to shore up its right flank against a surging One Nation before the March state election. This has resulted in the Coalition lurching to the right on issues ranging from gun control to law and order, and, in the case of the NSW Nationals, to abandon climate targets altogether.

As former opposition leader Mark Speakman resigned from the Liberals on Thursday – joining an exodus of MPs announcing they wouldn’t contest their seats in March – he warned his party against becoming “One Nation lite”.

But the move to the right has also created an opportunity for progressive independents running in urban seats usually held by Liberal moderates, with several candidates about to launch their campaigns.

This weekend, Amelia Loye, a management consultant who specialises in stakeholder and community engagement, will announce she is running in the seat of Willoughby, on Sydney’s north shore. She will attempt to oust Liberal MP Tim James, who holds the seat on a 2.6% margin.

Loye is backed by community group Willoughby Votes, which came close to securing an independent in the 2023 election with 47% of the two-party preferred vote.

“Willoughby has already shown it is ready for an independent,” she said. “Nearly half of voters backed one last time. I want to earn their support, and the support of those still deciding.”

She joins high-profile candidate Elouise Massa , a small business owner, who became a household name after campaigning for Northern Beaches hospital to return to public hands after her toddler’s death in emergency.

Massa will run in the Sydney seat of Manly against Liberal Adelaide Cuneo, who is seeking to replace the retiring MP, James Griffin.

The independent deputy mayor of Mosman, Michael Randall, announced last weekend he will run in North Shore as a community independent. Several of the people behind former independent federal MP Kylea Tink’s campaign are supporting him.

Monica Wulff, another independent, has announced a run in Davidson.

Voters on the northern beaches have already sent two independents to Macquarie street – and both have a good chance of being returned.

At the federal level, teal independents Sophie Scamps, Zali Steggall and Nicolette Boele hold McKellar, Warringah and Bradfield respectively. Tink held North Sydney for one term before the seat was abolished .

The NSW Liberal party is not giving up on the northern beaches and the north shore, despite the trend towards independents.

“We’ll keep making our case directly to voters, including why we oppose Labor’s gun buyback that targets law-abiding owners rather than criminals and terrorists,” a Liberal spokesperson said.

This time, however, the independents have a boost in the form of NSW Labor premier, Chris Minns .

During the final weeks of parliament before the NSW election, Minns joked that independent Jacqui Scruby, who won Pittwater at a byelection, should be known as “the member for Mona Vale road” for her tenacity in seeking an upgrade. It’s now being fast-tracked by the Minns government.

Minns has also appeared with Massa, and praised her for her advocacy.

But Minns, a shrewd political operator, has not fallen in love with independents – he’s just hedging his bets.

A year ago, with the Liberals languishing in the polls, Labor could have expected to increase its majority in 2027. But with One Nation storming to a primary vote as high as 30% in some polls, that prospect has dimmed.

In the current parliament, Labor has support of three independents – Greg Piper, Joe McGirr and Alex Greenwich – to ensure confidence and supply for its minority government. An expanded crossbench would give more options and provide a buffer for Labor losses to One Nation in western Sydney, the Hunter and possibly the Central Coast.

If all independents were successful – and that is a big if – almost the entire north shore region would go to state independents, except the seat of Wahroonga.

Most of the commentary about the 2027 election has focused on the likely Armageddon facing the Nationals, who could lose as many eight or nine of their 11 seats in the regions.

But a teal wave also remains a threat to the Liberal party, especially as the Coalition try to position themselves to fight off One Nation in the regions with policies that don’t sit well with urban voters.

One Nation could also prove helpful to independents if it makes good on its promise to run candidates in every seat. The party is more likely to shave votes from the Liberal primary vote, making it easier for the independents to overtake the Liberal on preferences from Labor and the Greens.

NSW’s optional preferential voting makes it hard to predict how strong flows will be – and it depends what parties advocate on how to votes. But in 2023 only 41% of One Nation voters nominated preferences, compared to 70% of Green voters and 49% of Labor voters.

The opposition leader, Kellie Sloane, has not ruled out either a preference deal or a post-election arrangement with One Nation, describing such options as “hypothetical”. But if they do end up preferencing One Nation, it may alienate moderate Liberals and ultimately work against them.

Disclosure: Anne Davies worked as a media and policy adviser to the independent federal MP Sophie Scamps from October 2023 to October 2024.
