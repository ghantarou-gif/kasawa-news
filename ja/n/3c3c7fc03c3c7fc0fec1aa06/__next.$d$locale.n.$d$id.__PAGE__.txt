5:T4f7,Vatican transparency about papal health has come a long way from the secrecy of the past.

But that does not mean Catholics were prepared for such seismic news about cancer and removal of a large section of Pope Leo XIV's lung - even after he announced on Sunday that doctors had discovered a nodule.

On Tuesday, the 71-year-old was diagnosed with lung adenocarcinoma following 90-minute surgery to remove a lower lobe of his right lung.

Part of the shock has, of course, been because there had been few signs until now that concern was needed about any imminent risks to the Pope's health.

A member of the Order of St Augustine, a saint who placed great importance on developing good habits, the Pope is known to place great importance in good habits, including when it comes to health and fitness, such as weekly games of tennis.

Now, with so many unknowns about the pontiff's prognosis and what treatment he might need, questions swirl around what may have to change in his routine, and for how long.
