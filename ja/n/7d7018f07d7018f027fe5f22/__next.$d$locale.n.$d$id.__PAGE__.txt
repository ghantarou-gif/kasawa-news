5:T4ed,Nearly 700,000 women across the UK are to be invited to join the world's largest trial of how artificial intelligence can help in breast cancer screening.

AI will be used to review mammograms to see if it can improve early cancer detection and ease the workload of clinical staff, while not over-diagnosing women unnecessarily.

Recruitment to the EDITH trial , external has already begun at Cambridge University Hospitals, the first of 30 sites across the UK.

Women aged 50 to 71 are invited for routine breast screening every three years in the UK, and more than two million attend annually.

At present two NHS experts look at each breast X-ray, known as a mammogram, but in the trial, one of the human readers will be replaced by AI.

Several smaller studies have already shown that AI can improve breast cancer detection rates, but researchers running this trial say they need to be sure it won't cause harm by leading to women being recalled unnecessarily for further tests.

Sian Taylor-Phillips, professor of population health at the University of Warwick, and co-chief investigator said: "AI-assisted technology could help improve early cancer detection but we want to avoid the overdiagnosis of milder disease that would never have caused symptoms."d:X
