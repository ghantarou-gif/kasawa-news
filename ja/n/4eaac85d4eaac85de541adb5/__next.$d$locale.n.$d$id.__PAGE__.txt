5:T562,The arrests of August Hanning , former president of Germany's foreign intelligence service, the Bundesnachrichtendienst (BND) , and his former chief-of-staff, Manfred D.*, are threatening to cause a major rupture in Germany's intelligence relationships, according to analysts, as the full scale of the scandal begins to be understood abroad.

Manfred D. is accused of selling around 2,000 highly classified documents to Hanning over a period of 12 years, long after the latter stepped down from the BND in 2005 following seven years as its chief. Manfred D. continued to serve all of Hanning's successors, including the current BND chief Martin Jäger, before retiring in March this year.

On one occasion, the state prosecutor said, Hanning "used classified intelligence provided by Manfred D. to draft an analysis for an officer of a foreign intelligence service."

According to Florian Flade, a reporter for public broadcaster ARD, most of these documents were weekly intelligence briefings the BND delivered to the German government, including the latest findings from partner intelligence agencies in other countries.
