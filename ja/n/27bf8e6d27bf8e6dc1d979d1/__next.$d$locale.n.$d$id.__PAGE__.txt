5:T4d1,World Athletics president Sebastian Coe hopes holding the 2029 World Championships in Nairobi will create a "legacy" for Africa.

The Kenyan capital will become the first city on the continent to stage the event, having been awarded hosting rights last month ahead of London and Rome.

After the East African nation failed with a bid for the 2025 edition, Coe reiterated it was "the right time" for World Athletics to tread new ground.

"All our instincts - and certainly mine - was to have that opportunity to build our sport to an even higher level in the continent," Coe told BBC Focus on Africa.

"The legacy behind it is important because it's a hard legacy of a new facility, but it's also the softer legacy, the human capital that you build up, because it's really important that countries are able to deliver those championships and more championships going forward.

"That's the legacy of experience and expertise."

It will be a big few years for Kenya, given the country will also co-host football's Africa Cup of Nations alongside neighbours Tanzania and Uganda in 2027.
