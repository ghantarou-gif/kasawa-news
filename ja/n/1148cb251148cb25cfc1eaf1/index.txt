20:Tcb0,The EU will next month impose its threatened caps on the number of Chinese hybrid cars that can be imported into the bloc, the European Commission has announced.

The quotas will also apply to hybrids from Japan, Turkey, South Korea and the UK, but the principal purpose is to limit imports from China after an agreement with Beijing in October that has led to a rise in exports to the bloc.

The quota, under World Trade Organization rules, is temporary and will apply for 200 days from 1 December during an investigation into imports of plug-in and battery-powered hybrid vehicles. The EU is attempting to cut its huge trade deficit with China.

“In view of the sensitivity of the hybrids sector, its strategic importance to the union economy in terms of innovation, electrification and employment, and the existence of sufficient evidence showing that imports of hybrids threaten to cause serious injury to the union industry,” the official journal said on Tuesday.

It will limit tariff-free imports of Chinese vehicles to 350,000 in the first year or about 191,000 over 200 days.

First-year quotas would be set at 310,000 for Japan, 230,000 for Turkey, 105,000 for South Korea and 190,000 for the UK, which last year exported 166,000 hybrids to the EU.

The quota amounts to about half of the number of Chinese hybrids expected to be imported to the EU in the next 12 months.

Figures in the journal showed that imports of Chinese EVs rocketed from 21,000 units in 2020 to 438,000 in 2024 when tariffs were imposed.

After the tariffs were imposed, imports of EVs began to “flatten out”, with a “clear shift” from EVs to hybrids from China , it said.

Worldwide exports of hybrids from China rocketed from 191,000 vehicles in 2023 to 1.124m in 2025, the EU said.

To head off “the likelihood of substantially increased imports into the union”, the EU now is imposing a quota.

The volume limits are “safeguard” measures that have to apply to all big exporters of ‌hybrid cars to the EU. Other countries to which the import caps apply are South Korea, Mexico, Morocco, Serbia and the US, but they are most restrictive for China.

According to industry reports, non-EU countries including the UK have questioned the allocations.

At a WTO goods council meeting last week, South Korea questioned whether the investigation was fair and transparent. Japan, the UK, Switzerland and Turkey stressed transparency and due process.

China’s commerce ministry said it hoped the EU would abide by the consensus the two sides had reached, and that it reserved all rights to firmly safeguard the legitimate interests of Chinese industries.

The European Commission said imports from China came not just at the expense of EU production, but were also eating into the market shares of other exporters such as Japan and South Korea.

The UK is still fighting against a 10% tariff on all exports of EVs to the EU, due to come into force on 1 January under the 2020 Brexit trade and cooperation deal.
