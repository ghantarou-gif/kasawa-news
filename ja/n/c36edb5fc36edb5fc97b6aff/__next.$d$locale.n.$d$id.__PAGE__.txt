Across Central America’s so-called Dry Corridor, farming families live at the mercy of the rains. In southwestern Honduras, Anastacio Martínez Gómez has spent most of his 73 years learning to work the land. But experience can only take him so far.

"When there’s no water, that’s where you run out of ideas,” he says.

In his village of Palagua, dry seasons can last five or six months. For years, a failed maize or bean harvest meant searching for work elsewhere.

Then, Mr. Martínez volunteered part of his land for a reservoir – opening up new possibilities for his whole community.

The Dry Corridor is a drought-prone region stretching across parts of Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador and Nicaragua, where more than seven million people do not have guaranteed access to food.

When crops fail, families face a stark choice: go hungry or migrate. This year, El Niño has made that choice harder.
