2e:T16a1,Israel has institutionalised the use of torture to extract confessions from Palestinian prisoners, sometimes documenting abuse in official records or granting advanced approval for “special measures” in interrogation, according to a report from the Public Committee Against Torture in Israel (PCATI).

This dangerous practice risked leading to irreversible miscarriages of justice, as Israel moved to implement a racist new death penalty law that targets Palestinians, the report said.

PCATI examined 42 cases in which Palestinians filed complaints about the use of torture by Shin Bet interrogators over the last decade.

More than three-quarters said they confessed after the use of “torture or other illegal methods”, and one in 10 provided incriminating statements about other people under torture.

“When it comes to Palestinian detainees, the Israeli legal system officially sanctions torture as a tool for extracting confessions, regardless of whether the content of those confessions is true,” said Sari Bashi, the executive director of PCATI.

“Torture as a tool of interrogation is particularly egregious given initiatives to impose the death penalty in Israel for the first time since 1962.”

Over the years, in hundreds of testimonies , Palestinian detainees have consistently described interrogations employing extreme sleep deprivation, physical violence such as slapping and kicking, threats, sexual violence and being held in stress positions.

Some of this abuse is also laid out in the official records of Shin Bet, Israel’s internal security service. PCATI details the case of a Palestinian lawyer arrested in February 2019 who was subjected to extreme sleep deprivation.

During an interrogation period lasting 27.5 days, or 660 hours, he was questioned for more than 552 hours, an average of 20 hours a day, with one session lasting nearly 48 hours. Some of the remaining 108 hours were spent travelling to and attending detention hearings.

In a 1999 ruling, Israel’s supreme court explicitly prohibited sleep deprivation as a deliberate interrogation method. Despite this, the lawyer was convicted of involvement in shooting attacks based on information extracted during this period.

The state inspector for complaints about Shin Bet interrogations (Mavtan) ruled that the interrogations took place in accordance with procedures, PCATI said.

More than 1,680 torture complaints were filed between 2001 and 2024 with Mavtan. None have led to an indictment.

Other cases are indirectly documented in Israeli judicial records, although judges rarely accept testimony about torture of Palestinians, PCATI said.

In one case, a detainee’s false confession, made under torture, was used as evidence by prosecutors. The man was released after his lawyer presented proof that he was not present at the scene of the crime, PCATI said.

Sleep deprivation was the most frequently used form of torture in complaints examined by PCATI, but more than half included the use of stress positions and other forms of physical violence.

Israel is a signatory to the UN convention against torture, but its legal system has effectively codified the use of violence and abuse where it is deemed “necessary” to extract life-saving information, PCATI said.

In a “necessity interrogation” or “military interrogation”, Shin Bet officials torture detainees “after consulting with their superiors and following an internal procedure for documenting their decisions and conduct”, the report said.

“[A] supposedly narrow exception ballooned and developed into a sprawling system of legal and administrative exceptions that swallowed the so-called rule, enabling the use of violence while maintaining the appearance of prohibition,” it continued.

There is no publicly available information on the scale of “necessity interrogations” inside the Shin Bet, PCATI said. They are an urgent concern as Israel moves to end a de facto moratorium on the death penalty, which had stood since the 1962 execution of the Nazi war criminal Adolf Eichmann.

“The death penalty is always wrong, because it’s cruel, irreversible and negates the right to life. No one should be executed, particularly not in a system tainted by systematic violence,” Bashi said.

In March, the Knesset passed a racist law allowing the execution of Palestinians convicted of terror charges over deadly attacks, but not Jewish Israelis who commit similar crimes.

In the occupied West Bank , where Palestinians are tried in military courts, execution is now the default punishment for a defendant found guilty of deadly terror attacks.

The legislation drew fierce international criticism, including from the UN’s high commissioner for human rights, Volker Türk, who warned that it would constitute a war crime if enacted.

A separate law empowers a special tribunal to impose death sentences in cases arising from the 7 October 2023 attacks.

Even in rare cases where courts have excluded confessions obtained under torture, Israeli case law allows prosecutors to use information derived from them as evidence to secure convictions.

PCATI said that as detention conditions worsened after October 2023, rights groups documented a sharp rise in violence by Israeli prison guards and soldiers , including beatings, attacks with dogs, electric shocks, threats and rape.

At least 103 Palestinian detainees have died in Israeli custody since 7 October 2023. Of 10 postmortems reviewed by Physicians for Human Rights Israel , five showed signs of physical assault and six showed evidence of medical neglect.

The Israel Prison Service said “all prisoners are held in accordance with the law”.
