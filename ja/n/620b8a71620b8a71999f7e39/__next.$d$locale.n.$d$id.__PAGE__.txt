U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy told a news conference that a contractor for the Federal Aviation Administration was working at a remote location to replace a copper line when an unknown issue overloaded the aging air traffic control system, forcing the FAA to keep planes on the ground nationwide.

The White House on Saturday shared more detail on the issue that took down air traffic control equipment on Friday, grounding flights nationwide for hours.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a contractor was doing routine maintenance to upgrade copper wire to fibre optic cable when something went wrong, causing an air traffic control communications outage.

He said the investigation will determine whether the fault was due to "incompetence" or "a faulty router."

Airlines across Canada and the United States are working to catch up on thousands of disrupted flights, with Air Canada's cancellations on Friday alone having affected 27,000 passengers.
