5:T4de,Music artist Chess Galea used to suppress any feelings of anger, fearing she would be branded as "hysterical" or "difficult to work with".

From the "mental load" women carry to sexism in the workplace or - worse - violence against women, Galea says "we don't realise how much we accept and how much we close our mouths".

But now she says she simply lets it out: "If someone catcalls me in the street, I rage. I don't care."

From the workplace to popular culture, experts say women like Galea, 34, are increasingly expressing their anger more openly than before.

"The key thing is under anger there's something we care about. Anger is a messenger, it brings us energy to protect, confront injustices, set boundaries, to make a change," says psychologist Dr Tara Brach.

What impact is that having on their professional and personal lives, and what can they do about it?

Psychotherapist Jennifer Cox, author and co-host of the Women are Mad podcast, started researching anger after suspecting her female clients' suppression of it was the root cause of some of their physical conditions.
