19:Ta74,Police investigating claims of widespread sexual abuse of children in Southampton have arrested more than 30 people.

One man is due to be sentenced in January after pleading guilty to sexually assaulting a 17-year-old girl, while another man will stand trial next month charged with raping a 15-year-old.

“We are conducting inquiries into concerns raised about men who were in contact with teenage girls,” a spokesperson for the Hampshire and Isle of Wight Constabulary said. “As part of these complex investigations, we have arrested 33 people. This has led to us being able to seize more than 80 mobile phones and any other relevant items which may assist in our continuing inquiries.

“While the information on those phones is reviewed, strict bail conditions have been put in place and our local policing teams have been monitoring those conditions.”

Detectives have been looking into the allegations over a lengthy period, with officers saying they have been working to “support and safeguard the girls, and build trust with them, which has allowed for formal reports to be made and statements taken.”

Hampshire police said one man, 22-year-old Sayeed Hashimi, had pleaded guilty to the sexual assault of a teenage girl at an address in Southampton in the early hours of 28 March. It was reported the girl had been “pinned down on a bed” before an attempt was made to forcibly remove her clothes, police said. Hashimi will be sentenced at Southampton crown court.

Aster Simon, 35, has been charged with sexual offences against a 15-year-old girl, including rape, attempted rape, sexual assault and making indecent photographs of a child. The alleged offences took place in the Portsmouth and Southampton areas in April and May, according to Hampshire police. He is due to stand trial at the same court.

Hampshire police said: “We take all reports of the exploitation of children extremely seriously. If you suspect a person of carrying out child criminal or sexual exploitation, or think someone you know has been a victim, please report this to us by calling 101, or by going to our website. If someone is in immediate danger of harm, please call 999.”

Southampton city council’s leader, Sarah Bogle, said the reports were “deeply disturbing”.

She added: “While it is important that we allow the police investigation and justice process to take their course, we want to reassure residents that protecting children and young people is our highest priority.
