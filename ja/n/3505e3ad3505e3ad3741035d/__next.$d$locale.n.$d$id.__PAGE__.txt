20:T118d,Nvidia “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO”, the chairman of the company’s board said Tuesday. Harvey Jones made the remark during Nvidia’s first quarterly earnings report since Jensen Huang’s ouster.

Nvidia announced the same day that its quarterly revenue beat expectations, coming in at $115.84bn, doubling from the same time period a year ago. The third-quarter earnings report comes just days after the world’s most valuable chip company saw the ouster of Huang over allegations of several extramarital affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and two with his own employees.

Following the tumult, Nvidia’s stock tumbled more than 10%, shedding hundreds of billions in value and dropping its valuation to $3.7tn. In October, the company had hit an all-time-high valuation of nearly $6tn, but its share price has steadily fallen since then amid a broader AI slowdown.

In after-hours trading on Tuesday, Nvidia’s stock ticked up 5% from earlier in the day.

Colette Kress, Nvidia’s chief financial officer, was named interim CEO last week and delivered the financial results on Tuesday.

Nvidia reported a revenue of $115.84bn and earnings of $2.68 per share, beating Wall Street’s expectations of $109.15bn in revenue and $2.47 earnings per share, according to Bloomberg analyst consensus. Nvidia’s revenue is now up 103% from the same time last year and up 20% over last quarter.

“We delivered another outstanding quarter,” Kress said during an earnings call with investors. “Our ambition will not change.”

When investors asked about negative effects on Nvidia’s talent retention in response to Huang’s ouster, Kress said she believed the company’s staff was “committed” and that “we will take care of our employees during this transition”.

When it comes to running the business, she said “it’s business as usual”.

Nvidia designs powerful computing chips used for artificial intelligence. The company controls about 90% of the chips market and the world’s top AI companies, including OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, rely on Nvidia for their technology. With much of Wall Street’s billions invested in Nvidia, the company’s twists and turns can have an outsized effect on the US economy.

Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and grew it from a niche firm geared toward video game chips to a trillion-dollar juggernaut at the center of the AI boom. As the CEO, he had about 60 direct reports, giving him control over most of the company’s daily functions.

Last week, following reports that Huang had a romantic relationship with an alleged Chinese asset, the CEO confessed to an extramarital affair. Days later, reports uncovered two more alleged affairs with company employees. On Thursday, Nvidia’s board of directors announced Huang’s resignation saying it was “effective immediately”.

Donald Trump and Silicon Valley insiders, including Elon Musk, the disgraced Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick and prominent venture capitalists, have called for Huang’s reinstatement as the CEO of Nvidia. On Monday, before departing on a trip to Asia, Trump told reporters he would not mind Huang’s return: “I would not mind that … I think he learned a big lesson.”

Huang issued his first public statement on Tuesday morning apologizing for his mistakes and thanking his friends and colleagues for their support. He also said he would be willing to return as CEO .

“If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” he wrote.

Elaborating on the position that Huang would not return, Jones said that the former CEO had voluntarily stepped down and the board accepted his resignation. Jones nodded to Kress’s 13-year tenure at Nvidia, saying she has been “at the center of this company’s extraordinary growth”.

“While this was an unexpected transition,” Jones said. “The board is proactively dealing with the consequences.”

Huang agreed on Tuesday to appear before the House foreign affairs committee, the select committee on the Chinese Communist party and the House intelligence committee for transcribed interviews in early December.
