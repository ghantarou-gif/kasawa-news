1a:Tac0,Costa Rica has barred entry to the UN special rapporteur on the Israel-occupied Palestinian territories, Francesca Albanese, earning the ire of the UN human rights chief.

Albanese, an Italian national, went to Costa Rica to receive an honorary doctorate from the University of Costa Rica. She was stopped at the airport in San José and not allowed to enter the country, and was transferred to a hotel before she travelled to Mexico on Saturday.

The UN human rights office said that Albanese had been stopped from entering the country on “national security grounds”. Costa Rica’s migration office said that its decision had been made after she was the subject of an “alert” over alleged ties to terrorism.

A protester outside Costa Rica’s embassy demonstrates against the decision to bar Francesca Albanese from entering the country. Photograph: Luis Cortés/Reuters

The UN high commissioner for human rights, Volker Türk, said that he was “deeply concerned” by the decision to deny Albanese entry. “This is difficult to reconcile with Costa Rica’s longstanding commitment to human rights,” said Türk.

Albanese shared a picture on X of an airport bench and her suitcase, where she was meant to stay the night. “Second night in the airport. Guarded by security at every step. Uncomfortable chairs. Noise. Bright light,” Albanese posted.

Albanese, who acts as an independent expert appointed by the UN human rights council, has been outspoken on Israeli abuses in Gaza and the West Bank . She has drawn ire from Israel and its allies for being one of the first, most prominent officials to accuse Israel of committing genocide in Gaza – which Israel denies.

The US placed sanctions on Albanese and revoked her visa for her work on Palestine in the summer of 2025, cutting her off from the global financial system. The sanctions make daily life a struggle for Albanese, who has no access to western banks or credit cards.

Albanese called for an investigation into whether her denial of entry into Costa Rica was connected to the US sanctions, speaking remotely at the ceremony for the acceptance of her honorary doctorate.

Costa Rica’s government, headed by the rightwing president, Laura Fernández, is close to the Trump administration and has cooperated with it on security and immigration issues. The government has also intensified its ties with Israel.

Israel’s envoy to the UN, Danny Danon, praised Costa Rica for its decision, accusing Albanese of using her UN position to spread propaganda against Israel.
