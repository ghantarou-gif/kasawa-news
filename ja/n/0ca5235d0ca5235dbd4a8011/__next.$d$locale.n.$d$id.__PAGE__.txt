After two weeks of tough negotiations, the EU said it could not support the proposed agreement, known as the Antalya Pact, put forward by the COP presidency, saying it does not respond to the science.

"We will not endorse a cover decision that does not even mention fossil fuels, the driver of climate change. And we will not accept negotiating backward in time," said Irish climate minister Darragh O'Brien, speaking on behalf of the EU on Sunday.

He said adopting a decision that omitted fossil fuels would be "unconscionable" and risked "deepening the chasm between global commitment and the action required."

Panama also rejected the deal, describing it as a step backwards in addressing the climate crisis, and adding that any decision must acknowledge "the truth about the collective failure on 1.5 degrees Celsius [2.7 degrees Fahrenheit]." That is the threshold outlined in the 2015 Paris Agreement to avert runaway climate change.
