Last week, endurance swimmer and ocean advocate Lewis Pugh completed a 15-kilometre swim across the Strait of Hormuz over three days to draw attention to the devastating environmental toll of this year’s conflict in the Middle East.

The UN Patron of the Oceans has undertaken extreme swims around the world – from Antarctic waters to a glacial lake on Mount Everest – to raise awareness of threats to fragile ecosystems. But this latest challenge, taking place in a conflict zone, was particularly extreme.

Driving him was the urgency of a mounting environmental crisis in the Gulf region sparked by the US-Israel attacks on Iran and counterstrikes from Tehran, following decades of pollution from oil and gas operations, industrial activities and desalination plants which have already put marine ecosystems under considerable strain.

Since hostilities rose amid attacks on shipping, oil pollution has risen sharply.
