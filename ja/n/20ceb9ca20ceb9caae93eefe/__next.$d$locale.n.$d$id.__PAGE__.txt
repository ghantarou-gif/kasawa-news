Each day the ceasefire in Oleshky holds means hope for civilians in the city, the UN’s top humanitarian official said, stressing that those wishing to leave must be able to do so safely and voluntarily.

“ Let this pave the way for vital aid to get in, and more humanitarian access on both sides of the frontline ,” Emergency Relief Coordinator Tom Fletcher said in a social media post , also relayed by the Secretary-General’s Spokesperson on Friday.

The ceasefire aimed at relieving the humanitarian emergency faced by trapped residents on the Russian-controlled left bank of the Dnipro, was brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump and announced by the White House on 23 October. It is due to end on the evening of 1 November.

Asked whether the UN had played any role in the evacuation of hundreds of people from Oleshky – close to the city of Kherson – spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric said it had not, but that it was providing humanitarian aid to evacuees on the receiving end, as they reach the Ukrainian side of the frontline.
