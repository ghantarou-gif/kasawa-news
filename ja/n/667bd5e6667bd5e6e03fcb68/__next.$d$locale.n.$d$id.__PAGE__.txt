That's exactly what millions of outraged East Germans experienced in 1977. But what led to this?

A poor harvest in Brazil saw global coffee prices skyrocketing, creating a major problem for East Germany . Unlike West Germany 's Deutsche Mark, the East German Mark was a domestic currency with no value abroad.

Even under normal circumstances, the country constantly struggled to obtain the hard currency it needed to pay for imports. In 1974 and 1975, East Germany spent around 150 million Valutamarks — the term used in East Germany for the West German Deutsche Mark — on imported coffee. The following year, it had to pay almost five times as much.

However, this rate of inflation could not be passed on to consumers because, "In the GDR, there was the dogma of stable prices — set by the state through law," explains historian Stefan Wolle, a consultant at the DDR Museum in Berlin (DDR is the acronym used for East Germany in German). "Prices were set by the Politburo, not by businesses. And they weren't allowed to change.
