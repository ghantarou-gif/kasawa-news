Has the world acted too late to stop catastrophic climate change? Image: Abriansyah Liberto/REUTERS

When world leaders agreed to do what they could to stop the world from overheating back in 2015, it was heralded as a victorious moment. Hope rippled from Paris, where the historic deal was made.

Since then, efforts to curb the use of planet-warming fossil fuels have achieved some success, with coal, oil and gas falling to a record low share of global primary energy supply. But they still account for around 86% of the world's energy.

"We're still burning stuff like [it's] the 19th century," Mark Maslin, professor of Earth System Science at University College London, told DW.

And that has consequences. The United Nations now says overshooting the temperature threshold agreed in Paris 11 years ago is inevitable. That means even more extreme weather events, like storms, flooding, droughts and deadly heat waves and resultant crop failures and water shortages across the world.
