More than half of West German respondents said reunification had no major impact on their personal lives. The remaining half is split: 19% said reunification had a positive impact, while 18% said it had a negative one.

Germany held three state elections in September, two of them in eastern states. They were seen as a test of nationwide voter sentiment. The results were particularly devastating for the center-right Christian Democrats (CDU), the party of Chancellor Friedrich Merz .

In Saxony-Anhalt and the city-state of Berlin, where it had been in government, the CDU dropped by 10% compared to the previous election and lost its hold on power. In Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania , the party even failed to cross the 5% threshold for representation in parliament. This has never happened before in Germany's history.

Merz has long been unpopular and has seen his support fall even further in the aftermath of the elections. The coalition he leads, comprised of the CDU, the conservative Christian Social Union (CSU) and the center-left Social Democrats (SPD), is unpopular. Only one in 10 respondents in the survey said they were satisfied with the federal government's work and the chancellor's performance, a historic low.
