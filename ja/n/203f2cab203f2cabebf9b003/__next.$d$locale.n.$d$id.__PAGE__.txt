Suddenly, the silence is shattered: Several helicopters circle the night sky, and flashing blue lights illuminate the darkness. A long convoy of vehicles moves through the streets. Soldiers stand atop armored Bundeswehr vehicles, their machine guns at the ready. For local residents, it's an unusual, perhaps even unsettling sight in an otherwise peaceful port city.

"Red Storm Charlie" is the name of the exercise that brought parts of Hamburg, northern Germany's largest city, to a standstill for three days. The Bundeswehr, police and civilian organizations were jointly testing how well-prepared they are to respond in a crisis.

The scenario centers on a deteriorating security situation on NATO 's eastern flank. NATO troops, represented in the exercise by Bundeswehr soldiers, arrive at the port of Hamburg, Germany's largest seaport, before moving eastwards through the Hanseatic city.

Acts of sabotage and demonstrations, also included in the scenario, hamper their advance. With around 1,700 participants, including 500 soldiers, the exercise held in late September was the largest of its kind to date.

"Red Storm Charlie" forms part of a classified strategy known as the " Operationsplan Deutschland " (Operations Plan Germany). Through this document, the Bundeswehr is preparing for the possibility of a Russian attack on NATO territory .
