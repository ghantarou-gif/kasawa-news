Israeli police say he was arrested "concerning ties to an act of terrorism".

Authorities have requested what is known as "administrative detention", which allows the Israeli state to detain people indefinitely without charge on the basis of secret evidence that they cannot see.

"We definitely think this is a political arrest," said Dr Yael Berda, one of Tunsi's university professors.

Tunsi, a Palestinian resident of occupied East Jerusalem who works with well-known Jewish-Palestinian campaign groups, was arrested a month after Israel held an election that saw Arab-led parties win a record 19 seats in parliament, the Knesset, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing coalition lose its majority.
