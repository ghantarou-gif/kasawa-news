1b:Tb95,Jensen Huang apologized to staff on Tuesday for the tumult he has caused at Nvidia. The company’s CEO acknowledged he violated the code of conduct for having extramarital affairs, but pushed back on the reports that Donald Trump was instrumental in his reinstatement as chief executive.

“I made some terrible mistakes,” he said, according to a livestream reviewed by the New York Times . “I was unfaithful to Lori, and I hurt my kids and, of course, I hurt all of you.”

The apology came one day after Huang returned as CEO of Nvidia , and less than two weeks after he was ousted from the role. Following a whiplash series of events, the company’s board of directors reinstated Huang on Monday, despite his admission that he had had an affair. News reports allege the CEO had romantic relationships with two company employees and a suspected Chinese spy.

During Tuesday’s apology, Huang told staffers he was in therapy and that it was “not acceptable” that he violated the company’s code of conduct. In an impromptu speech to employees on Monday, Huang said he was “terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this” and that he was “going to be a better CEO the second time around”.

While Huang apologized for his affairs, he rejected narratives that he was a “tech bro” who was “above the law”, according to the New York Times. He added that it was “completely untrue” that Trump pressured Nvidia’s board to reinstate him. The board changed course, he said, because it “evaluated the entire situation” and “gathered facts”. Huang said Trump assured the board that he was not a national security threat.

The US government has been investigating whether Megaspeed , a Singapore-based company formerly run by Huang’s alleged ex-lover, Alice Huang (no relation), diverted Nvidia semiconductors to China in breach of export controls. US government officials have also raised concerns about Alice Huang’s alleged role as a Chinese asset.

Trump reportedly dialed into Nvidia’s Sunday board meeting to advocate for Huang’s return, according to Semafor . Nvidia’s board publicly said on Monday that it reached its decision to bring back Huang with “input from senior levels of the United States government”.

Huang potentially faces two congressional hearings next week, one before the House foreign affairs committee and one before the Senate Banking committee. Gregory Meeks, a Democratic congressman from New York and ranking member of the House foreign affairs committee, said on Tuesday that despite Huang’s reinstatement as Nvidia’s CEO, a hearing should still take place.

“The American people deserve to hear publicly from Mr Huang about how potentially billions of dollars’ worth of our most advanced technology wound up in the hands of our greatest competitor, and what role this administration played,” Meeks said.
