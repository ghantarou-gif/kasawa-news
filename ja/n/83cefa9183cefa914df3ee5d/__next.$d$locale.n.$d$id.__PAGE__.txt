1f:T1307,An Apple Watch on sale for $680. A pair of women’s Hunter yellow rainboots discounted for $127, and a pair of men’s UGG boots, $180. A Carhartt beanie just shy of $20.

These discounts – all reportedly featured last year on Amazon’s own curation of its best Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals – sound like good deals for the holiday gift season. But a recent analysis from progressive thinktank Groundwork Collaborative found these products and others had been listed at the same price or even less just days before Amazon started advertising them as discounted.

Overall, researchers found that about 30% of the products Amazon highlighted for its Black Friday and Cyber Monday campaigns were priced the same or even lower in the month before the sale. That share increased to 60% when extending the timeline to 60 days prior to the sale events. Across the board, median sale prices were actually nearly 20% higher than their pre-Black Friday offering.

Those Hunter rainboots, for example, were listed for nearly $50 less – about $80 – just nine days before going on its advertised “sale” Groundwork said. The Apple Watch and men’s UGG boots were both listed for roughly $80 less within a month of the Amazon’s Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales of last year.

It was the “most rigorous, intensive project” that the thinktank had taken on in its history, Lindsay Owens, the president and chief executive of Groundwork, said after the report’s release.

“Americans are really squeezed right now. Back-to-school shopping was expensive. Buying a Thanksgiving meal for your family is going to be expensive. Holiday gift shopping is going to be really expensive,” Owens said in an interview with the Guardian. “Sale events are a really important way that families stretch their dollars, and Americans trust that these Amazon sales events and other sales events around Black Friday and Cyber Monday are worth waiting for, and offer them genuine savings.

“Our report suggests otherwise,” she continued, “that the sale events aren’t always what they’re cracked up to be.”

Amazon said in a statement on its website that the Groundwork Collaborative published “deeply flawed and false claims” and that it “cherrypicked” findings.

“This is the second attempt that Groundwork has made in the past week to assert false claims about our deal events based on flawed methodology and inaccurate data, and it’s clear they have no interest in the facts and are focused on pursuing a pre-determined narrative,” said in the statement.

In further statements to the Guardian, Amazon said that it analyzed deals from the report’s claims and found that 98% of the products in 2024 and 99% of products in 2024 were at the same price or lower over the past 30 days from the sale events.

“When we looked at the research’s actual deals for 2025, we found that 89% of the products had lower prices and 10% were the same, compared with the prior 30-day low prices,” the company said. “Groundwork’s research appears to focus on two low-trafficked corporate blog pages that inadvertently listed some products as deals and was clearly marked as subject to change. Groundwork is basing this misleading claim on a handful of these products that were mistakenly included on those pages but were not badged as deals in our store.”

For products that were accurately labeled as deals, consumers saved an average of more than 35% off list price, the company said.

The report comes as Americans say they are facing higher prices across the board, especially for gas and groceries. Companies have also come under increased scrutiny over unfair pricing practices, including dynamic and surveillance pricing, which Owen has further scrutinized in her recent book, Gouged.
