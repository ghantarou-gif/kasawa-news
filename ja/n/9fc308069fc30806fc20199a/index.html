One Nation finds its base in regional Victoria

4 時間前

Shortly before the election polls closed on Saturday, Chris Burson was standing with a trio of his fellow One Nation candidates outside a Geelong polling booth.

The team was excited to see just how well they would do in the count, particularly following pre-election polling predicting the rise of One Nation.

They were equal parts confident and circumspect. A primary vote explosion seemed likely, but so too did difficult preference flows.

One Nation enjoyed a surge in voter support in regional Victoria on election day. ( ABC News: Jeremy Story Carter )

"I think there's a real vibe that things are going to change in not just Victoria, but Geelong," Mr Burson, the party's lead candidate for Western Victoria, said.

"It's really exciting," he said. "I mean, this is history, really."

As one of the slowest, and most complicated election night counts neared midnight on Saturday, it would appear Mr Burson was right.

One Nation had asserted itself as the most disruptive force ever seen in a Victorian election, largely driven by its rise in the regions.
