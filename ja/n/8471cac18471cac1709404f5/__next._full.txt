35:T25ce,A short two years ago, Elon Musk stood before an audience of tens of thousands of people in New York City and proudly told them: “I’m not just Maga. I’m dark, gothic Maga.”

On Wednesday, Musk spoke with much less conviction. “I’m not Maga,” he said. “Well, at least I’m not sure what Maga means.” The richest person in the world added he felt “foolish” for spending some $500m to elect Republicans in the midterms. “If I could rewind the clock, I would have spent less money,” he said at the New York Times’ DealBook conference, adding that he did not have “some sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican party”.

Silicon Valley’s tycoons made a hard-right pivot during Donald Trump’s second term, courting the president both on X and in private meetings, donating millions to his inauguration and, later, to his pet project, the White House ballroom. Musk himself spent more than $200m through his political action committee, America Pac, to help elect Trump in 2024. The attempts to cozy up to the president appeared to work. Trump took office in January 2025 flanked by some of the very executives who once banned him from their platforms in 2021. Musk, for his part, was installed as the head of the so-called “department of government efficiency”, a newly conceived and temporary federal initiative.

After Democrats’ decisive triumph in the US midterm elections, though, many of the tech barons are retreating.

The CEO of Palantir, Alex Karp, was among the first to attempt a pivot, softening his previously hardline stance on immigration a week before Musk in an interview with Politico. The animated leader of the tech firm that has powered Trump’s deportation apparatus said that the president mishandled immigration enforcement.

“I was really happy to hear the president be honest that – you know, his words, I believe, were something like: ‘Yeah, we kinda went too far,’” Karp told Politico. “And I’m glad he’s saying in public now what I’ve been saying in private.”

Karp, who once described the company as “anti-woke”, broadened his retreat from immigration to right-leaning politics in general.

“I’ve been a Democrat my whole life. I grew up in a far-left environment. I know more about being a progressive than almost anybody on the planet, and I absolutely believe Palantir – all of our products are essentially progressive,” he said.

Karp’s latest comments stand in stark contrast with Palantir’s work with the US Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency (ICE), functioning, according to Lesley Nash, a staff attorney at immigration defense organization Just Futures Law, as the “corporate and data analytics backbone of ICE”.

“Look, we power ICE,” Karp said in an earnings call in November 2025. “We power efforts to defend America and Ukraine. And with allies. We’re on the frontline of all adversaries, including vis-a-vis China. And we support ICE, and we’ve supported Israel. I don’t know why this is all controversial, but many people find that controversial.”

“Our ability to win while sticking by what we believe is important for the world,” he added in the call.

Other Silicon Valley leaders had also changed their tones in the immediate aftermath of the midterms.

Alex Karp, the Palantir CEO, on the Claman Countdown at Fox Business Network studios in New York City on 27 July. Photograph: John Lamparski/Getty Images

Mark Zuckerberg, the CEO of Meta, is reportedly showing signs of shifting left. Kaplan, who has been at Meta for 15 years, was promoted at the start of the Trump administration and was known for his ties to the the Republican party.

Many Silicon Valley CEOs have helmed their companies for years, and it’s not their first time maneuvering the changing tides of American politics .

Though a self-described moderate, Musk previously donated to the Democratic national committee as well as Barack Obama’s re-election campaign in 2012. In 2018, he celebrated Tesla achieving a perfect score on an index of LGBTQ+ equality in the workplace for a fourth year in a row.

“Don’t buy our car if that’s a problem. People should be free to live their lives where their heart takes them ❤️🧡💛💚💙💜🖤💖🌈,” he wrote .

Eight years later, having moved from California to Texas and donated hundreds of millions of dollars to Republican candidates, Musk struck the opposite tone. He fear-mongered about the Democratic candidate for Senate in Texas, by tweeting that James Talarico “will trans your kids”.

Responding to a question Wednesday about Texas’s Democratic governor-elect, Musk said, “I don’t think she’s sort of a far-left governor, and based on everything I’ve heard about her, I think everything will be fine in Texas with Gina [Hinojosa] as governor.”

He described her as “quite moderate” and characterized his own politics the same way.

Kaplan replaced Nick Clegg, who, as a former deputy prime minister in the UK, had taken a more global approach. When Meta was just called Facebook, the company had championed diversity, with a goal of ensuring half its workforce was from underrepresented backgrounds. Zuckerberg and other leaders started a group to help immigrants. The company also temporarily banned Trump’s Facebook account after the January 6 insurrection.

Under the first Trump administration, Palantir worked closely with ICE’s investigative unit, developing software that helped the agency investigate and arrest people using a searchable super-network of government and private databases.
