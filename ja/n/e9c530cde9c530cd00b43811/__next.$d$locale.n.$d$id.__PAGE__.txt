5:T5b5,The heads of Germany's intelligence agencies rarely provide information openly. What members of the Bundestag 's Parliamentary Control Committee discuss in their bug-proof meeting room with the presidents of the Federal Intelligence Service, or Bundesnachrichtendienst (BND), and the Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution (BfV), as well as the president of the Military Counterintelligence Service, is strictly confidential.

Since 2016, however, a public meeting has been held once a year, open to journalists, as well as citizens who register in advance. At this year's meeting, held Monday , intelligence officials seemed extremely alarmed. They painted a picture of a country in the midst of a confrontation with Russia .

"The military scenarios envisioned for the year 2029 have already become a reality today in the shadowy world of intelligence," BfV President Sinan Selen said, referring to a recent assessment by NATO experts that, under certain conditions, Russia could be capable of launching an attack on the alliance in a few years.

BND President Martin Jäger said there were no indications of an imminent military attack. Instead, Moscow is waging a "shadow war" through sabotage, cyberattacks, disinformation, and espionage.
