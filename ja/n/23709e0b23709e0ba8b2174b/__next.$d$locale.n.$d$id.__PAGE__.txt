The chemical tanker that sank on Wednesday with 7,000 tonnes of diesel aboard will be towed “immediately” to the Spanish port of Cartagena despite environmental concerns, Spain’s transport ministry said on Friday.

The Maltese-flagged Med Emre, which capsized between Valencia and the holiday island of Ibiza, is expected to reach the Spanish port as early as Monday 30 November.

“After checking the structural integrity and seaworthiness of the ship, the towing operation to a safe port will begin immediately, taking advantage of the favourable weather conditions,” the ministry said in a statement.

The port, in an industrial area of the southeastern region of Murcia, met the depth, size and safety requirements for unloading the fuel, the ministry said. With conditions at sea liable to worsen, Cartagena “offers a calm environment” close to pollution response, logistics and petrochemical facilities.
