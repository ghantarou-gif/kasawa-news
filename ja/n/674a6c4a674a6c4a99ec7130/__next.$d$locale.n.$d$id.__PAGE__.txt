Norway has agreed to buy carbon credits from Indonesia and Morocco to help finance new renewable energy projects in the two countries.

The agreements cover up to 12 million tonnes of emissions reductions in Indonesia and 8.78 million tonnes in Morocco, over a 10-year period for each programme, Norway’s climate ministry said on Monday (16 November).

Both deals were signed at the COP31 UN climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye. The Indonesian agreement was signed on 11 November, followed by Morocco’s the next day.

Under the arrangements, Norway would pay for emissions avoided by generating electricity from renewable sources. The income from selling carbon credits is intended to help fund projects that would not otherwise be built.

Norway will only pay once the emissions cuts have been achieved, independently verified and the credits transferred. The reductions must go beyond what would have happened without the programmes.

The Indonesian agreement was signed by PLN, the country’s state-owned electricity company, and the Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI), acting on Norway’s behalf. Indonesia relies heavily on coal for electricity and is seeking to expand renewable generation.d:X
