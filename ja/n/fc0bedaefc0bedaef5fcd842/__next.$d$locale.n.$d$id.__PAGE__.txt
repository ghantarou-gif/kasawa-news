From aid convoys in conflict zones to passenger jets and cargo ships far out at sea, safe journeys depend on GPS, satellite navigation and old-fashioned radio transmissions. But these signals are increasingly being blocked or faked – and civilians are paying the price.

Those signals travel through the electromagnetic spectrum, which also underpins modern military operations and has become a battleground in its own right.

The more we rely on these technologies, the more exposed we are to interference, manipulation and disruption, Federico Mantellassi, a researcher in the Security and Technology Programme at the UN Institute for Disarmament Research (UNIDIR) has told UN News .

The two main threats are jamming, which blocks signals, and spoofing, which feeds systems false ones.

UNIDIR estimates suggest GPS spoofing in civil aviation rose by 500 per cent between January and August 2024, affecting hundreds of flights.

In early 2026, the crisis in the Middle East saw the highest concentration of maritime jamming on record.
