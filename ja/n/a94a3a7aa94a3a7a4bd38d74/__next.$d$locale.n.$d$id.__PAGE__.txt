20:Tec1,A would-be Reform UK MP told an undercover reporter that Muslims could not be British and asylum seekers should be put in “concentration camps”.

The comments were recorded as part of the undercover investigation by ITV that has already seen Reform accused of “appalling behaviour” and “extreme racism”.

Reform has said it had opened investigations where activists or members had “clearly behaved inappropriately”, adding there was “no room for racism” in the party.

In footage due to be broadcast on Sunday, a Reform activist and aspiring MP, who has yet to be publicly identified, described himself as “pretty hardline about Islam”.

He said: “If a Muslim’s prepared to de-convert, I’m happy.

Reform UK’s home affairs spokesperson has described himself as a ‘British Muslim patriot’. Photograph: Guy Corbishley/Alamy Stock Photo/Alamy Live News.

“But if they want to remain faithful to Islam, then this would be, I would have disagreements, especially with, um, I mean, as far as I understand, [Reform home affairs spokesperson] Zia Yusuf is a Muslim … to me … you cannot be a Muslim and British … it’s like oil and water.”

Discussing people crossing the Channel in small boats, the same activist said: “In the first week of election, build concentration camps, and everyone who comes across on the Channel, you’re in there.”

Excerpts from the investigation released earlier in the week included councillors and candidates within Reform using racist language.

In one excerpt, black children were referred to as “animals”, while in another a Reform member said his area had become “infested” with Asian families.

Bridget Phillipson, the Labour party chair, said Nigel Farage’s response to the revelations showed he was “completely unfit to lead”.

She said: “Bad apples don’t fall far from the tree. The sleaze, scandals and appalling revelations of racism and rank misogyny piling up by the day go right to the top of Reform UK.

“Nigel Farage is incapable of taking responsibility and getting his house in order because he himself shares culpability.”

Speaking earlier in the week, Farage said that while Reform carries out vetting “you can’t vet people for what they say in the future”.

In a statement to ITV, Reform said: “Reform UK has improved and prioritised its vetting procedures, but from time to time, a handful of individuals who do not share our values will initially make it through vetting.

“This is the same for all organisations. However good the vetting, one cannot vet a future comment.

“There is no room for racism in Reform UK. We always respect due process of investigations and whilst these are ongoing it would not be appropriate to comment on individual cases.”

On Friday, Alan Feeney resigned as a Reform councillor for Solihull and Birmingham after racist comments he had made were uncovered in the investigation.

Alan Feeney resigned as Reform councillor after racist comments he made were uncovered. Photograph: ITV

ITV revealed secretly recorded footage of Feeney making racist comments about Asian homeowners, calling them “parasites”, and stating that “no more than 1% of [the] British population can be non-white”, during a local election campaign.

In a subsequent Facebook post, Feeney wrote: “There is no excuse for my comments. With responsibility comes accountability, and it is for that reason that I have resigned every post I hold on both Solihull borough council and Birmingham city council.”

He added: “My remarks were wrong, offensive and completely unacceptable.
