Like most kids who shouldn’t have been playing Grand Theft Auto III in 2001, Eamonn Swayah imagined a version of Rockstar Games’ groundbreaking criminal sandbox set in his own back yard. In his case, that was a Manchester council estate, complete with local accents, driving on the left, and plenty of ways to hustle.

He didn’t know it, but swapping the game’s facsimile of early 2000s New York City for a different setting was an idea he shared with many watchful game developers. Rockstar’s 3D open world template, it turned out, could be plonked in just about any location, so long as it had people and enough cars to run them over with. After GTA III attracted immense commercial success (and controversy), dozens of copycats and spiritual sequels hit the market throughout the 00s and 2010s.
