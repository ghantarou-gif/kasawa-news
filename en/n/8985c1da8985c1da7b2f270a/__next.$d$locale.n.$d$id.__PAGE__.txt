5:T525,Top U.N. court rules Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya : NPR

Top U.N. court rules Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya The International Court of Justice found that Myanmar security forces "acted with genocidal intent" against the Rohingya during military operations in the northern Rakhine state in 2017.

Rohingya refugees watch the International Court of Justice ruling over whether Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya Muslims, on a screen at a camp in Bangladesh's Cox's Bazar on Nov. 19.

The United Nations' top court ruled Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority, the first time in the court's 80-year history that it has held a state responsible for the crime.

Judges of the International Court of Justice voted 9-6 that the military's 2017 crackdown was carried out with intent to destroy the long-persecuted Muslim minority.

Myanmar's military-backed government on Friday dismissed the court's ruling as "unfair," according to The Associated Press. Khine Khine Soe, a spokeswoman for President Min Aung Hlaing, was quoted saying that Myanmar would make its position known after reviewing the court's decision.
