22:Tf4a,More than 30 labor union leaders from 19 countries are calling on governments to review their public contracts with Amazon and Amazon Web Services, citing the company’s poor record on worker rights and collective bargaining, in an open letter published on Thursday.

“Amazon’s consistent record of disrespect for workers’ fundamental rights should disqualify it from being awarded public contracts – its behaviour is fundamentally inconsistent with the shared democratic values of our countries,” the union leaders wrote.

The letter cites Amazon’s anti-union activity around the world, including over a decade of strikes by workers in Germany, its decision to shut down its Quebec warehouses after a warehouse voted to unionize in 2024, allegations of retaliating and silencing workers in Australia and legally challenging labor laws in the US and Canada .

In the US, the Institute for Local Self Reliance estimates cities, counties and school districts spent $2.2bn on Amazon in 2023.

Amazon has also secured several multibillion-dollar contracts with the US federal government in recent years, including a nearly $2.6bn award from the Department of Homeland Security in 2026 and a 10-year contract with the National Security Agency that was renewed in 2022, reportedly worth up to $10bn.

“Our governments continue to award Amazon billions in public contracts for cloud computing services underpinning critical public infrastructure,” the leaders wrote. “This pattern reveals a dangerous disconnect between public procurement practices and the democratic values our societies are meant to uphold, while creating unnecessary risks for public services.”

Signers from the US include Stuart Appelbaum, president of the Retail Wholesale Department Store Union (RWDSU); Milton Jones, international president of the United Food and Commercial Workers; Claude Cummings Jr, president of Communications Workers of America; Sean O’Brien, president of the Teamsters; April Verrett, president of the Service Employees International Union (SEIU); and Jonathan Smith, president of the American Postal Workers Union.

Christy Hoffman, general secretary of UNI Global Union; Lana Payne, national president of Canadian trade union Unifor; Gary Smith, general secretary of the UK’s GMB; and Frank Werneke, president of ver.di in Germany also signed the letter.

“Every public contract is a choice about what governments are willing to pay for. Right now, taxpayers in country after country are paying Amazon while it shuts down warehouses, busts unions and tries to get labour laws struck down,” Hoffman said in a statement. “Governments set the rules for who gets public money. They can require that companies respect workers’ right to organize and bargain, and they should start with the biggest contractor that refuses to.”

The letter comes as the UK Home Office is planning to renew its Amazon Web Services (AWS) cloud computing contract for a possible cost of £881.6m over two years.

Governments could reform public procurement rules to make certain worker rights standards mandatory for contractors along with establishing enforcement mechanisms that would exclude corporations that violate workers’ rights from future public contracts.
