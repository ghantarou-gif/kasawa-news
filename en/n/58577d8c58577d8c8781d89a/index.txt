22:T112e,I find myself on the wrong side of gaming history this month. I have zero zeitgeist. My takes are as fresh as something found at the rear of the fridge. I haven’t even played Grand Theft Auto VI yet, but we’ll get to that.

Worse, all month, the Nintendo sector of the gaming world has been going googly eyed over Zelda. The Ocarina of Time remake seems to have produced more joy than all the puppies in the world. But not for me. I have never liked Zelda. It’s like being a movie reviewer who doesn’t like Disney, or a vegan shark.

I have played the Zelda games, because #1 Child was into them, but not to any decent extent. Shallow Male Me back then felt that it was for little kids, and worried that testosterone would leak from me every time Link cut some grass with his sword instead of slicing something’s head off.

Determined to right this wrong, I made something of a misstep. While everyone else was trying to play Linkin Park on the ocarina in the new remake, I dusted off the Nintendo Wii that I rediscovered while moving house. In the disc slot lurked The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess, a cheaper alternative to spending 50 million quid on a Switch 2. Finally, I would discover the lure of Zelda.

Stuck trying to leap over tiny platforms … The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess. Photograph: Nintendo

The opening level was everything I feared. Link was running around looking for fish to give to cats. But the Wii control system made even this simple task hell. Walking along thin ledges with that diddly nunchuck and no 360-degree camera control is like being drunk on a tightrope. On a unicycle. After 10 hours of gameplay, I was still pushing the wrong button among what feels like an ocean of them.

It’s not all bad. The graphics are beautiful. The reveal of the Twilight World and accompanying lycanthropy is spectacular, and so existentially poignant it should be written in French. Wolf Link and his assorted moves are also terrific, or at least they would be with a different controller. (I really should have dug out a GameCube instead.)

But I was having a frustrating experience, far removed from the unbridled joy flooding my socials from people playing the OoT remake. Then I hit my first dungeon. And I remembered why I really disliked Zelda games: I hate dungeons. They are mazey mental torture prisons with switches, locked doors and repetitive puzzles. You must find a specific solution or you cannot continue. I am an open world guy! I want to have different ways to crack a nut, including a sledgehammer option.

While I tried to leap over tiny platforms to cross a lake of lava in the Goron Mines so that I could pull a chain to move a block to run back the way I came to get on a platform before it retracted, GTA VI came out. Grand Theft Auto is a game that I have loved in all its incarnations. I felt like the rest of the world was driving round in Ferraris while I was on a penny farthing.

The Ferrari to Dominik’s penny farthing … GTA VI. Photograph: Rockstar Games

Meanwhile, after dying in a dungeon, getting confused and going the wrong way, I finally found the door that led to the big boss. But I couldn’t open it because apparently I had not collected all three key shards. I could swear that I did! After a lot of backtracking, I returned to the boss door and attempted to save the game so that I didn’t have to go through this again, but instead my useless fingers slipped and I selected “quit game” instead of “save game”. When I loaded it up again, I had been returned to the dungeon’s entrance. I was profoundly depressed by this.

I just don’t get Zelda, at least not this one. Maybe I really should have spent all that money and tried the new Ocarina of Time. So why continue? Because I want to belong. I want to be part of this gigantic community that finds such joy in Zelda. That’s what so much of gaming is: connections with other people, from global Fortnite Discords to the Norwich Amiga Group.

We all want to be a part of something. Thankfully, there’s another huge thing going on in gaming, and it’s definitely more my speed: I fire GTA VI up and it is everything I hoped for. I am … home. I may never be part of the Zelda crowd, but right now we have two massive games dominating global culture, two huge communities of friends we haven’t met yet.
