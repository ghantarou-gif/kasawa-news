AI demand will push up interest rates around the world, IMF head warns

Nov 19, 03:15 PM

Booming demand for artificial intelligence will force the world’s most powerful central banks to raise interest rates, the head of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) has warned.

Kristalina Georgieva, the managing director of the Washington-based fund, said demand for AI hardware and related technology products was pushing up consumer prices around the world.

She said that, alongside the energy shock from the war in the Middle East, this was adding to inflationary pressures around the globe.

“This calls for a tightening of monetary policy in many economies,” she said.

Speaking at the 2026 Apec summit in Shenzhen , Georgieva warned that both factors would push global inflation to 4.7% this year. She added that the rapid rollout of AI was also contributing to stronger than expected economic growth in several countries, despite the headwinds from the Iran war.

Central banks have been forced to raise interest rates this year after the outbreak of war in February drove the global oil price above $100 a barrel, reigniting inflationary pressures around the world. The European Central Bank (ECB) raised rates in June for the first time since 2023, followed by the US Federal Reserve in September and the Bank of England earlier this month .

Financial markets expect further increases in the cost of borrowing next year, as the rapid rollout of AI bolsters economic growth against the volatile backdrop of the conflict.

However, global oil prices have fallen back in recent weeks, following the US midterm elections and an upswing in the supply of crude . Fears about an AI bubble and febrile conditions in global equity markets could dent business investment, and economists warn of a slowdown in demand as high inflation squeezes households.

Georgieva said that while the energy shock would eventually dissipate, “AI is for ever” – making it a significant driver of world trade, competitiveness, productivity and economic growth.

AI hardware and related tech products now account for more than one-tenth of the world goods trade, a share that is rapidly rising, she said. But some countries were benefiting more from investment than others.

“To put it simply: you snooze, you lose,” she added. “Countries need digital infrastructure, labour markets and workers that are ready for AI, and risk mitigation – especially to control financial stability and cybersecurity risks.”

Georgieva’s comments came a day after the ECB expressed concerns about the rising amount of debt that AI companies are taking on to fuel their growth.

The central bank said there were “historic parallels” with the dotcom bubble that led to a tech market crash at the turn of the millennium, as well as the sub-prime mortgage bubble that set off the 2008 financial crisis. It said in its financial stability review that any fallout from an AI crash “could lead to spillovers affecting euro-area financial stability”.

For now, at least, the sector is still booming. Thursday marked a pivotal day for AI as Anthropic debuted on the US stock market , raising the largest investment of any initial public offering after pricing at $83 per share.

The company, which makes the AI chatbot Claude, is expected to become one of the most valuable publicly traded businesses in the world at $1.5tn, despite reporting losses of $80bn in the first half of 2026.
