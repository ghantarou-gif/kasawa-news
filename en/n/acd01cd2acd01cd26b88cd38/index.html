Demands for post-poll justice as Tanzania accused of weaponising counterterrorism laws

Oct 29, 12:25 AM

Families affected by election-related violence in Tanzania that led to the deaths of at least 500 people a year ago have told the BBC they want justice for those killed in the protests and help with medical expenses for those injured.

"We can no longer afford to pay hospital bills," Meshack Sane, who was shot in the leg, told the BBC.

On the eve of the anniversary of the poll that saw President Samia Suluhu Hassan elected with 98% of the vote, rights group Amnesty International also accused her government of "weaponising counterterrorism laws to silence critics and instil fear".

The opposition dismissed the result as a "mockery" of democracy as Samia's two main challengers were excluded.

Tundu Lissu, the 58-year-old leader of the main opposition Chadema party, had been put on trial for treason ahead of the poll - preventing him from standing.

He had been demanding electoral reforms and a verdict in his case is due next week.

Earlier this week, Lissu went on a hunger strike as he was not being allowed access to his lawyers - an allegation the prison authorities denied.

His lawyer Dickson Matata told the BBC on Wednesday he had now been granted a meeting and would be eating again.
