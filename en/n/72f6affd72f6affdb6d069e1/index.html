Long-deployed USS Lincoln servicemembers reunited with families

yesterday

On Thursday in San Diego, servicemembers who have been on an extended deployment on the USS Abraham Lincoln were reunited with family members.

The USS Abraham Lincoln is back in its home port of San Diego this morning. The aircraft carrier just finished a nearly 11-month deployment, which is the longest time spent at sea without a port visit. John Carroll from our member station KPBS reports on its return.

JOHN CARROLL, BYLINE: Thousands of families were on the pier waiting, cheering, holding signs and ringing cowbells as the Lincoln approached.

CARROLL: Kailah Clark had been waiting with her siblings since early morning.

KAILAH CLARK: We're waiting for our dad. He's a master chief, and his name is Lavell Clark.

CARROLL: The Clarks said they'd heard the stories about the Lincoln, news reports of plumbing problems, insufficient supplies, less-than-desirable food.

CLARK: We were very shocked. We were like, what? Like, the ship? Like, that's actually crazy. But, you know, I'm just glad they're returning now, and things can probably get better for them, you know what I mean?

CARROLL: Just after noon, the ship, which had been in sight for about a half hour, was slowly guided into the pier by tugboats.
