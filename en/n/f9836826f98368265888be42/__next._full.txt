23:Tef9,The UK sent a Sri Lankan asylum seeker who had been convicted of sexual assault to Eswatini, a southern African country to which he had no connection and where he killed himself.

Kishanth Peramalya was part of a group that set sail from India hoping to reach Canada and claim asylum there. The boat was rescued by the Royal Navy in October 2021 when it got into difficulty and brought to Diego Garcia in the remote British Indian Ocean Territory, which hosts a US-UK military base.

In November 2024, the UK allowed 61 migrants to enter from Diego Garcia after a damning UN report about conditions in the camp. However, the then home secretary, Yvette Cooper, denied Peramalya entry to the UK due to his criminal convictions while he was on Diego Garcia for four counts of sexual assault, one count of assault occasioning actual bodily harm and one count of arson. Cooper’s decision was upheld by UK courts.

Peramalya had been granted protection from being returned to Sri Lanka, having been tortured and sexually abused by Sri Lankan military personnel as a teenager. In late March, the Maldives said it would take him for 30 days, according to the BBC . He was then transferred to Eswatini, a country landlocked by South Africa and Mozambique and formerly known as Swaziland, where he killed himself on 13 September.

A draft deal between the UK and Eswatini governments would have committed Downing Street to cover the costs of Peramalya’s resettlement there for three years, according to the BBC, which said the UK was also finalising arrangements for Peramalya to live in Eswatini permanently.

The Guardian has not seen the draft deal. Thabile Mdluli, a spokesperson for Eswatini’s government, said: “The initial arrangement was temporary in nature and intended to safeguard Mr Peramalya’s safety while the government of the United Kingdom worked towards a long-term solution regarding his circumstances.”

She said that no formal agreement was ever reached over Peramalya’s long-term residency status with the UK and declined to comment further on the specific arrangements of his stay in Eswatini or the circumstances around his death.

Mdluli said: “Eswatini has not received any other similar request from the government of the United Kingdom. The government of the Kingdom of Eswatini remains committed to ensuring that any agreements entered into with external partners safeguard national interest as well as the safety and security of Emaswati [Swazi people].”

A UK government spokesperson said: “Keeping the British public safe will always be our first priority, and we will continue to take the decisions necessary to protect the British people. That includes taking steps to ensure high harm foreign criminals cannot enter the UK. We continue to work with the Eswatini authorities in relation to this case.”

Peramalya had post-traumatic stress disorder and attempted suicide multiple times on Diego Garcia, according to a UK high court judgment in February 2025.

Peramalya’s family told the BBC he was initially housed alone in an isolated guesthouse in Eswatini. “My brother did not want to go there at all,” said his sister, speaking from India. “He couldn’t manage being there alone, he was very lonely.”

She said he had called her four times a day and was initially having weekly psychiatric appointments, which were made daily when his mental health deteriorated, as well as having someone brought in to prepare his food.
