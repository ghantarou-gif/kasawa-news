High-security prisoner used soap to escape despite being handcuffed and chained to officer

3 hours ago

A prisoner from one of England and Wales’s most dysfunctional high-security jails is believed to have used soap to help escape from officers while visiting a hospital for treatment.

The 36-year-old detained at HMP Manchester managed to slip away despite being cuffed and chained to an officer, sources told the Guardian. He is believed to have lathered his metal cuff with liquid soap and slipped his hand out while being given privacy to use a bathroom.

After 13 hours of freedom and a short pursuit on foot, the offender was detained and arrested by Greater Manchester police officers.

The incident has sparked a formal inquiry by the Ministry of Justice (MoJ) and comes amid an increase in escapes from jails in England and Wales. This is the second time this year that a prisoner has escaped after attending a Manchester hospital.

Marwan Jumaa, a 20-year-old Sudanese national deemed to be a risk to himself and others, was serving an indefinite secure hospital sentence for attacking two women. He escaped after being transferred from his secure hospital facility to North Manchester hospital for medical treatment in July.

Mark Fairhurst, the national chair of the prison officers’ union, the POA, demanded that more officers should escort offenders from category A and B prisons to hospitals.

“This incident highlights the risks that officers face when escorting prisoners to and from hospitals. There should be a minimum of three officers on every hospital appointment instead of two under current rules,” he said.

The number of prisoners escaping from custody has steadily increased over the past three years. In 2025-26, 14 people escaped from custody across England and Wales, compared with 12 the year before, and nine in 2023-24.

HMP Manchester, once known as Strangeways and which houses category A and B prisoners, has been said to have some of the worst conditions across the England and Wales estate.

HMP Manchester is located close to the city centre. Photograph: Joel Goodman/The Guardian

The category B prisoner escaped on the evening of 20 November after two prison officers accompanied him to a local hospital.

According to prison escort regulations, category B prisoners must wear “double handcuffs” – where both wrists are restrained close to each other and a prisoner is cuffed to a member of staff.

Subject to risk assessment, the double cuff can be adjusted to a single cuff and a ratchet chain when privacy and hand movements are required, such as when using the toilet or shower. This allows the prison officers to give an offender privacy in a room or cubicle while still being attached to an officer with a chain, which can be more than a metre long.

Sources said that the prisoner had been placed in a single cuff and chain but had escaped from his cuff while using a bathroom. Police were alerted at 8.30pm and searched for the man overnight. He was located at 9.30am on Saturday, and was detained and arrested after attempting to flee on foot.

The internal MoJ investigation will focus on whether safety procedures were carried out at all times and if the man was handcuffed properly.

A spokesperson for Greater Manchester police said: “We received a call shortly before 8.30pm on Friday 20 November that a 36-year-old man had absconded from lawful custody whilst at hospital.

“Officers deployed and utilising specialist resources conducted a full search over the course of the evening and into the morning. The man was safely detained at around 9.30am on Saturday 21 November following a short foot chase and further arrested on suspicion of escaping lawful custody. He has been transported back to custody.”

Prison sources said the escape of a prisoner held in a high-security prison was still relatively rare. In September 2023, Daniel Khalife, 23, sparked a high-profile search when he broke out of HMP Wandsworth by clinging to the underside of a food delivery truck. At the time he was being held on remand for spying charges.

Last year, an escaped prisoner tried to flee armed police by ramming them with his getaway car. Jay Bennett, 33, was serving a nine-year sentence when he absconded from HMP Sudbury in Derbyshire.

An official report into HMP Manchester conducted earlier this year by the prisons watchdog found large quantities of drugs flown in by drones, an obvious rat infestation and violence against prisoners and staff.

The report by Charlie Taylor, HM chief inspector of prisons, said the jail was “in a precarious state”. In 2024, he invoked an urgent notification, which automatically triggers ministers to intervene in the jail’s management.

A Prison Service spokesperson said: “This individual is now back in custody. We have launched an investigation into this incident and any prisoner who is unlawfully at large faces extra time in prison.”
