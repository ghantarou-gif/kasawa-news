23:Tdca,A leader of India’s youth-led Cockroach movement has asked the Delhi high court to give him permission to fly to the UK after immigration officials at Delhi airport stopped him boarding a flight early on Tuesday morning.

The court is scheduled to hear Saurav Das’s case on Thursday morning. His lawyer asked the court for a quick hearing because Das has by now already missed some of his engagements at British universities and can only attend the other events on his itinerary if he is allowed to fly out as soon as possible.

Das, 27, is one of the main faces of the Cockroach movement, which shot into the political firmament out of nowhere in May to challenge the Narendra Modi government with street protests.

The movement succeeded in forcing the education minister to resign in July over exam leaks and has since functioned as a pressure group.

Das had been invited by the Oxford Union Society to participate in a “fireside chat” on the movement.

Indian student groups at Edinburgh, Cambridge, Queen Mary University of London and the London School of Economics have also invited him to speak.

“Students want to know how we succeeded without any violence, how we began and where we are going. There is a great curiosity about the movement,” Das told the Guardian.

Reports in the Indian media have alleged that Das was informed well before the flight that his passport contained discrepancies.

“None of these reports is true,” said Das. “I received an email five hours before my flight saying that I should not use my passport for travel. It gave no reason. And I did not see the email until I arrived at Delhi airport. What’s more, I’ve travelled abroad before on this passport since it was issued in 2019 so what’s the problem with it now?”

He suspects that the government is using a frivolous case filed in Chennai against him as a pretext. The case has been filed, Das surmises, by someone hostile to the Cockroach movement and disputes his citizenship.

Das believes he was deliberately stopped by a government that is “scared” of someone such as him speaking abroad about the ideals of Mahatma Gandhi and Jawaharlal Nehru (independent India’s first prime minister) and that it wanted to make an example of him.

“It’s a chilling signal to young Indians that we can do what we like, that if we can do this to a leader of a movement, we can do this to you too,” said Das.

In recent years, the government has stopped foreign speakers and academics, including several from the UK, from entering India.

Last year, Francesca Orsini, a professor at Soas University of London, was stopped at Delhi airport and deported despite holding a valid visa. The government said she had been blacklisted for allegedly conducting research and speaking at events while visiting on a tourist visa in 2024.

Also last year, Nitasha Kaul, a professor at Westminster University, was detained at Bangalore airport and deported for “anti-India activities”.

Some people who find themselves blacklisted have chosen to fight back. In 2023, the UK-based journalist Amrit Wilson had her overseas citizen of India card which allows free movement into India cancelled, also for alleged “anti-India activities”.

She took legal action and earlier this week, the Delhi high court ruled that the government had failed to give any concrete details of what it meant by “anti-India activities”.
