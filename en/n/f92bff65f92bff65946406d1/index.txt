1e:T9b2,India’s second-richest man, Mukesh Ambani, is launching what could be the country’s biggest-ever initial public offering (IPO), despite a stock market downturn.

Mumbai-based Jio Platforms, a telecoms company backed by Meta Platforms and Google, is hoping to raise up to 302bn rupees (£2.4bn) and is targeting a 10.3tn rupees valuation when it floats on the stock market from Wednesday.

Set up a decade ago as a subsidiary of Ambani’s conglomerate Reliance Industries, and led by Akash Ambani, the oldest son of patriarch Mukesh, Jio offers low-cost broadband to millions of Indians.

Jio secured 212 anchor investors on Monday before its blockbuster IPO. They include some of the world’s largest fund managers, such as: BlackRock; Singapore’s sovereign wealth fund; the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority; Fidelity Investments; Schroders; JP Morgan Asset Management; Franklin Templeton; Invesco; and Prudential.

On Wednesday, shares will be offered to retail investors, and the IPO is set to close on Friday.

Reliance Industries said in a stock exchange filing last week that Jio had set a price range of 1,065 rupees to 1,119 rupees a share for its maiden share sale, offering up to 270m new shares.

The family-owned conglomerate has been encouraging its 400,000-plus employees to buy shares, offering them a discount of 54 rupees a share.

At the top end of the price range, Jio would be worth 10.3tn rupees, which would make it India’s fourth-largest company, behind its parent Reliance Industries, Bharti Airtel and HDFC Bank. It would be more than twice as much as the 4.9tn-rupee valuation in 2020, when Meta and Google invested in the company.

However, the top valuation is far below initial expectations of more than £110bn when the telecoms group posted a draft prospectus in the summer.

It comes amid a broader stock market slowdown in India, with the blue chip Nifty 5o index down more than 12% this year.

Isha Ambani, who sits on Jio’s board as a director, told investors last week that the business idea was spawned by the poor connectivity she and her brother Akash faced when they returned to India on a break from studying in the US in about 2009-10. She struggled to submit a college assignment online because the internet was not working.
